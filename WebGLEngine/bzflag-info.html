🛡 BZFlag — the world file, the geometry, and the tank

What this is

A clean-room port of BZFlag's world format and tank physics into this engine. The grammar and the constants were read from the 2.4 branch's src/bzfs and src/common/global.cxx and re-implemented. Nothing is copied, and BZFlag's own maps are not vendored — the map that ships here is original.

Built in the same order as the Endless Sky port: a parser, an adapter, and a coverage instrument that says out loud what has not been done. Then geometry that a test can prove without a GPU. Then something that drives around inside it.

The buttons are in server.html, under the BZFlag tab. Start a rendezvous room, launch two GPU Brain pilots into it, watch their kills and losses, and open the viewer already pointed at the right room. There is also a probe that connects bz/net/bzfsClient.mjs to a real bzfs as an observer and reports what the server said — it never spawns a tank and never sends a position. Those routes spawn processes, so they answer 403 to anyone who is not the host, an authenticated session, or a LAN peer the operator has exempted.

Drive against the brain, by hand. Start a rendezvous server, start a pilot, and open the viewer with ?url=http://host:8788&room=arena. Bots are tinted, so you can tell the GPU Brain from a person. It cannot yet join a real bzfs server — that is 56 message types over a TCP/UDP hybrid, and a round of its own.

The GPU Brain drives one of these. node brain/bzPilot.mjs --dry-run shows the whole loop without touching a network; point SWEK_BZ_URL at a rendezvous server and two of them will hunt each other. The room, the presence packets and the owner-addressed damage rule are ev/presence.js and ev/esAuthority.js — unchanged, and written for a game about spaceships.

Coverage — what the adapter ignores

Built first, not last. The guard in bz/tools/bzw-coverage.mjs --selfcheck checks the tables against the adapter source in both directions: a declared keyword the builder never quotes is over-claiming, and a keyword reported as ignored that the builder plainly reads is under-claiming. It scans the raw source, comments included. A coincidence of vocabulary trips it. Move the word, not the guard.

loading…

Point it at a real map to see a real number:

node bz/tools/bzw-coverage.mjs path/to/hix.bzw
node bz/bzw-build.mjs path/to/hix.bzw out.json     # bakes the coverage report into the artifact

Getting BZFlag's own maps

Not vendored. They belong to BZFlag and ship under its licence.

git clone --depth 1 -b 2.4 https://github.com/BZFlag-Dev/bzflag
node bz/tools/bzw-selfcheck.mjs      bzflag/misc/maps
node bz/tools/bzw-geom-selfcheck.mjs bzflag/misc/maps
node bz/tools/bz-tank-selfcheck.mjs  bzflag/misc/maps

Then drop any .bzw onto the viewer.

What is proved, and what is not

checkwhat it settles
bzw-selfcheckthe grammar, BZDB's expression language, the world model. 108 checks.
bzw-geom-selfcheckevery box and pyramid is a closed, outward-wound solid enclosing exactly the volume arithmetic says. 66 checks.
bz-tank-selfcheckcollision against an independent polygon oracle; a jump reaches v²/2g and no higher; a tank never ends a frame inside anything. 81 checks.
bz-mesh-selfcheckthe mesh family: containsPoint exactly as upstream has it, a mesh cube's volume, a tetra as a sixth of its box, per-face flags. 84 checks.
bz-protocol-selfcheckthe fifty-six message codes, and exact bytes for everything the client sends — worked out by hand from the source, because a codec that round-trips against itself proves nothing. 90 checks.
bzfs-loopback-probethe real client against a real TCP server: handshake, framing, and every negative path. 21 checks. Nobody has pointed it at a real bzfs.
bz-shapes-selfchecka sphere divisions 1 is an octahedron enclosing exactly (4/3)abc; an N-sided cone is exactly a third of its polygonal base times its height. 111 checks.
bz-pilot-selfcheckthe pilot never fires through a wall, never wedges itself, keeps the counterfactual a learner needs. 72 checks.
bz-net-selfchecka tank on the wire: a tank's id names its owner, a hit is reported and not applied, a replay lands once, a peer who leaves is not a kill. 45 checks.
bz-live-probetwo real brain/bzPilot.mjs processes, a real rendezvous server, an owner-addressed kill on the wire — and a headless peer playing the browser's part, killing one of them. 25 checks.
bzw-coverage --selfcheckthe coverage report cannot drift from the code, in either direction. 15 checks.

Not proved, because it cannot be, headlessly: what the viewer looks like. Everything it draws was settled before it drew anything, which is the reason for doing it in that order.

Recorded, not simulated

Every object keyword in a .bzw is read. Four of them — textureMatrix, dynamicColor, physics and weapon — are recorded: parsed, kept by name, inspectable, and nothing animates and nothing shoots. Calling them handled would flatter us; calling them ignored would be the other lie. The report prints two percentages: how much is read, and how much of it is simulated.

Two facts about mesh collision are reproduced rather than improved upon: MeshObstacle::inBox ignores the tank's box and tests only its midpoint, and containsPoint returns false for every point in the universe if the mesh declared no inside or outside point. A port that quietly fixes its source is a port you cannot trust about anything else.

A group transform that is not a rigid motion — a shear, a non-uniform scale — cannot be expressed as a position and a rotation. Its geometry is exact; its collision is the axis-aligned lie of its local box. Those obstacles are flagged approx and the world counts them.
