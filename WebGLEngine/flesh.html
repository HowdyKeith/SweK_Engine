Flesh

Box3D has no soft bodies. It does not need any — it is the skeleton, and this is the skin.

This build cannot do joints

What you are looking at

The same eleven bones as ragdoll.html, simulated by Box3D as rigid boxes. Every frame, the boxes are converted to segments — a bone is a segment with a radius, not a point — and a capsule distance field is marched into a surface.

Nothing here is sculpted, and nothing is stored between frames. The skin is a pure function of where the bones are right now, which is why two machines running the same recording grow the same skin. A deterministic skeleton wearing a non-deterministic skin would throw that away at the last step.

Drop it

grid 28 blend 0.16

The cost, measured here, live

A 60fps frame is 16.7ms for everything — physics, skin, and draw. Pull the grid slider up and watch the skin eat the budget: the field is cheap and the march is not, and cells scale as the cube.

—

What this is not

Three skins. The hybrid is the default because it measured best. Take the shape from the capsules (smooth, analytic, cheap) and the bulge from the fluid: field = capsule - strength * max(0, rho - rest)/rest. Where the flesh is crowded the field drops and the surface moves outward -- because the particles really are crowded, not because a spring was tuned to pretend. At rest the bulge is zero and it is the capsule skin (measured: 856 triangles against the capsule's 844, 1% apart).

Measured on the dev box, 28-cell grid, 500 particles, real 11-bone rig:

spring lag2.8ms904smooth, no volume
raw SPH22.8ms1212volume, but lumpy AND over frame budget
hybrid14.3ms968smooth AND volume, fits 60fps

The fluid step is 10.7ms of that 14.3 — the cost is the physics now, not the skin, which is where it should be. The lumpiness of the raw SPH skin is the kernel width, not the particle count: 400 particles gave 1408 triangles, 900 gave 1424. More particles never fixes it.

Two skins, and neither is the upgrade. The spring lag draws a smooth capsule union and fakes weight with a damped spring per bone: 904 triangles for about 2ms. The SPH particles are a real fluid -- 500 of them, bound to the bones, with pressure -- and the skin is their actual density. It costs roughly seven times as much and it is lumpier at any resolution that fits a frame. It buys exactly one thing, and the spring cannot do it at any stiffness: volume. Fold a limb and the particles in the crook have nowhere to go, so the pressure rises -- measured at +14% mean density on a 115-degree fold. Watch the density readout while you shove it.

The honest budget (measured on the dev box, JIT warmed): 500 particles is ~11ms of physics, and the skin sampling is ~3.4ms at an 18-cell grid, ~6ms at 22, ~21ms at 28. Pull the grid slider up in SPH mode and it stops fitting in a frame. That is not a bug to fix later; it is what a real fluid costs.

It is a lag, not a simulation. Each bone's flesh trails its own motion by a damped spring, so a limb that stops abruptly has its skin keep going for a few frames and settle. That reads as weight. It is not pressure, volume preservation, flesh colliding with flesh, or anything an FEM would give you — and you still cannot push the skin independently of the bone under it. This engine owns two real solvers (gpu/ClothSim.js, physics/sph/sph.js) and this is neither. Calling it a soft body would be a lie; it is a skin with lag. That paragraph is about the spring mode, and it is why the SPH mode exists.

The state is in one place, deliberately. boneField is a pure function of the bones and stays that way — the spring lives in front of it, in fleshDynamics.js, stepped once per tick at a fixed dt. Integrated with a variable frame delta instead, two machines would grow two different skins by frame 300, and nothing on screen would look wrong.

The blend is a trade, not a free win. It rounds the silhouette as well as filling the creases. Turn it to zero and you can see every capsule; turn it up and the figure inflates into a snowman.
