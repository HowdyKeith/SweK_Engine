The same chaos, asked two ways

The logistic map at r=4 is deterministic and chaotic. Framed as x(n+1) vs x(n) an exact law exists and must be found. Framed as x(n) vs n there is none, and nothing must be returned. Same numbers, opposite required answers — a regressor that always finds an equation is decoration.

A verdict with a spread, not a boolean

The fits above cut the data once, at 60%. So the verdict is a property of the data and of where it was cut — and nothing separated the two. Rolling the origin refits at several cut points and reports the distribution. For a real law the recovered exponent is the same from every window, so its spread collapses to nothing; that is a sharper claim than a high R², because it says the law does not depend on which part of the range you looked at — which is what a law means.

What this does not claim

Sparse regression does not invent the vocabulary. Hand it a library containing x^2 and it can find x^2; hand it one without and it cannot. What it genuinely does is decide which of the offered terms matter and with what coefficients. The library is an input, and the physics knowledge hides there. These simulations are also noiseless, which makes this easier than real measurement — a success here does not transfer straight to laboratory data.
