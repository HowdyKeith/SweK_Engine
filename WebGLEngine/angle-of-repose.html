The angle of repose — two solvers, one closed form

A block on a slope slides when tanθ > μ, so the critical angle is atan(μ). Two completely independent solvers bisect for it here — a rigid-body wasm solver (box3d) and an XPBD particle solver — and neither is told the answer while it searches.
This page exists because asking for it found a defect. physics/mechanics/contactKeys.mjs imports node:url and the Node box3d bootstrap at module scope, so no browser could ever have imported it — the second instance of a fault first found at v3617. The arithmetic moved to physics/mechanics/reposeOps.mjs, which has no imports at all and takes the wasm module as a parameter; its gate asserts the two are the same function object rather than two copies that agree today.

Run it

Both solvers land above the key, and they do not agree with each other. Measured headless: box3d is out by 3.52% / 2.24% / 1.59% in tanθ at μ = 0.3 / 0.5 / 0.8, the XPBD solver by 0.146% / 0.100% / 0.082%. A solver that scattered either side would just be noisy; one that is always high has a mechanism. I expected that mechanism to be the detector and it is not — tightening the speed threshold 33× moves box3d's excess by nothing at all, so the 2.2% is its own friction cone, not a measurement artefact.
Past μ = 1 the fixture stops being valid, and the sign flips to say so. A unit cube tips at 45° (tan = 1) whatever the friction is, so above μ = 1 the geometry takes over and atan(μ) is no longer the right key for this block. Drag μ past 1.0 and watch box3d fall below the line — not a smaller error, a different mechanism.
