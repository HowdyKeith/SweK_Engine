Four phase changes, four keys — and none of them comes from this engine

Keith asked whether voxels could melt, gasify, freeze, burn or crystallise. Four of the five were built and graded at v3618–v3621; burn was refused, with its reason recorded. This page is the front door all four have been missing.
This page exists because a red gate finally got read rather than excused. deviceInstrumentMap has listed melt, freeze, vaporize and crystallize as unexplained since v3618 — each device either overlaps an instrument or is exempt with a stated reason, and these were neither. The cheap answer was four exemptions. That would have been wrong: an exemption says never meant to have a front door, and the real cause was that all four modules import { pathToFileURL } from "node:url" at module scope for their main-block guard, so no browser could resolve them — the third instance of a fault first found at v3617 and again at v3630. The arithmetic moved to physics/thermal/phaseOps.mjs, which has no imports at all; its gate asserts the two are the same function object rather than two copies that agree today.

1. Melting — the only phase change with an exact answer

The one-phase Stefan problem gives X(t) = 2λ√(αt) with λ solving λeλ²erf(λ) = St/√π. Nothing in it comes from our grid. The solver evolves enthalpy and derives temperature, which is the correctness argument: the latent-heat stall falls out of the state variable instead of being a case somebody remembered to write. The claim is the convergence order, not an error — a single error number would pass on a solver that is wrong and never improves. Press negative control to run the obvious temperature-based solver instead: it does not merely run ahead, it puts every cell above Tm, so there is no front at all.

2. Freezing — not melting run backwards

True of the equation, false of the physics: heat crosses the liquid to reach a melting front and the solid to reach a freezing one. Ice/water freezes about twice as fast as it melts at the same |ΔT| — and the ratio decomposes into two factors pulling opposite ways, neither of which predicts it alone. The control is the part that makes it a finding: give both phases identical properties and the two directions must agree to the digit, so the asymmetry is carried entirely by the materials and none of it by the freeze branch.

3. Gasification — mass, volume, density: pick two

The expansion ratio is a function, not a number — three orders across conditions anybody would call ordinary — so there is no constant to build a grid around. A voxel storing a type fixes the density of whatever sits in it, which leaves exactly three choices and each is wrong in exactly one quantity by the same factor r: there is nothing to trade. The control is the critical point, where r goes to exactly 1 and all three coincide: the trap is distance from critical, not the voxel scheme. Drag the slider up to 647 K and watch it arrive.

4. Crystallisation — the exponent is geometry

Avrami's exponent is set by counting dimensions — 3 for site-saturated growth in three dimensions, 4 under continuous nucleation, the extra power being the nucleation rate integrated over time. Nothing in it comes from the lattice. The seed spread is measured before the claim, not after, which is the measurement ising.L was missing: the two predictions are one apart and the per-seed spread is several times smaller, so the ensemble discriminates the two modes and a single seed does not. Watch the per-seed column — the worst site-saturated seed and the best continuous one are under two per-seed sigma apart.
