The count detects an absent term

The previous round graded bonds and angles by a counting law and left the obvious gap: dihedrals.js and improper.js were in neither fixture. Four atoms closes it — 3N−6 = six vibrations and six zero modes for a non-linear tetratomic.
And the count does something the separate gates cannot: it notices a term being absent. Drop the torsion and rotation about the j–k bond costs nothing — a free internal rotation is an extra zero mode, so the count falls to 3N−7. Drop the improper and the umbrella goes free the same way. Each term's own gate checks its force against a finite difference of its own energy; neither can notice the term missing from a molecule, because there is no molecule in it.

1. Both minima are constructed, not converged

Torsion: all four atoms in the xy-plane, so φ is exactly 180°. With n = 3 and γ = 0, V = kd(1 + cos 3φ) has cos(540°) = −1 — a minimum, gradient zero, second derivative +9kd. Improper: coplanar means the signed volume V6 = a·(b×c) is exactly zero, which is its target.

2. Remove a term, gain a zero mode

The torsion adds exactly one mode and touches none of the others — the five surviving frequencies agree to better than 10−12. A term that shifted the other five would be coupling where it shouldn't. And the improper result was not what I expected: the three in-plane angles look like they should hold the centre flat, and they don't — measured, not assumed.

3. A symmetry nobody asked for

Two exactly-degenerate pairs plus two singletons — 1 + 1 + 2 + 2 = 6, the spectrum of a threefold-symmetric planar centre. That's a consequence of the geometry the fixture was built with, not something any term was told to produce. The tolerance is the fixture's own error bar: my first check demanded the splitting be under 10−12 and it went red at 5.2×10−12 — below the Hessian's own measured asymmetry, which is to say at the instrument's floor. hessian() already returns that error bar; the comparison is against it.
