The limiter at a refinement seam

A refinement interface is non-uniform spacing, and this engine already had one. The previous round proved a limiter monotone on a Cartesian cell and named the untested case in as many words: with non-uniform spacing the offsets are not ½ and the argument does not run. So this should break.
It does not break — and the measurement also showed which guarantee I had been watching. Monotonicity survives, for both spellings, at every refinement ratio. The reason is one line: minmod already bounds its output by the smaller of the two differences, and half of the smaller difference cannot leave a neighbourhood whose half-span is at least that difference. A factor of two of margin, independent of the widths.

1. Two spellings of the same limiter

What breaks is accuracy, and only at the seam. The interiors are bit-identical for both spellings at every ratio — so a whole-domain error number would have called this fine. At the seam the naive spelling degrades by 2.7×, 3.9×, 4.4× the interior at ratios 2, 4, 8, while the correct one sits below the interior, because the seam cells are finer and a correct reconstruction gets that for free.
And on a uniform grid the two are identical to the last bit, which is why the defect is invisible until it isn't: a difference is the slope times the spacing when the spacing is one number. The fix is a unit, not a tolerance — divide by the centre-to-centre distance (hi+hi±1)/2, which across a seam is neither width, and apply the slope over hi/2.

2. The numbers

The penalty saturates. 4.91e-3 at ratio 4 against 5.66e-3 at ratio 8 — the first step away from uniform costs almost all of it, and refining harder costs almost nothing more. Not claimed: that this shape is present anywhere in this tree; no file here is accused of it. Not tested: triangles, which is still the case the previous round's scope note names and which this round does not close.
