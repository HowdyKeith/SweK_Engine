Changing the grid under a live field

An adaptive grid refines where the action is and coarsens behind it as the action moves on. A wave crossing a moving refinement window takes one round trip per crossing — refined, then coarsened back onto the grid it started on. In the continuum that is the identity: same grid in, same grid out. Everything measured here is what the arithmetic lost on the way.
The first thing this found was a check of mine that could not fail. Refine-then-sample reads exactly zero for every field — including a sine at eight cells per wavelength — and it reads that by construction, because the operator pair is a left inverse. "Sampling is lossless" would have been a true sentence about function composition and a false one about physics. The real cost is not in the round trip; it is in what the refined field is, graded below against an analytic field neither operator is told.

1. The two ways back

Sample keeps the value and loses the sum; average keeps the sum and loses the value. Averaging conserves the discrete integral to round-off — the whole reason to choose it — and pays by damping, because a conservative remap is a low-pass filter. It is not even exact on a straight line, where sampling is. Two correctness claims, one operator satisfies each, and neither satisfies both.

2. The orders, and the verdict

Refining costs a second-order error once. Coarsening back conservatively costs a first-order one every time — and it accumulates linearly, with no saturation. A moving refinement window that crosses a wave N times injects N first-order insults into a scheme whose entire accuracy claim is second order. That is the number you would want before building an adaptive grid, and it is the one their README cannot give.
