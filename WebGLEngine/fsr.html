What each pane is. The scene is a continuous function, so a render is a point sample and the ground truth is an 8×8 area average — the “infinite sample” reference. Bilinear and FSR1 get one unjittered frame. The temporal pane gets a new jittered frame every tick: jitter → render at the low resolution → jitter-aware resolve to display resolution → reprojected accumulation, then RCAS.

What to look for. On the zone plate the temporal pane climbs past every spatial result — it recovers detail the render grid cannot carry. Measured here at 2×, 32 frames, against bilinear’s 14.82 dB: 21.23 dB static, 16.95 dB panning. Panning gains less because each frame resamples the whole history through a bilinear reprojection, and that blur is a running cost a static camera never pays.

The controls are where the mechanism shows its edges. On smooth the temporal pane loses to bilinear (68.54 against 72.13) — there is nothing beyond Nyquist to recover, so accumulation buys nothing and the reprojection’s own resampling still costs. On the pixel checker all four panes sit at 5.50 dB with a static camera: the checker alternates every display pixel, the render grid is half that, and sub-render-pixel jitter never crosses a checker cell — every jittered frame is the same frame, so there is nothing for the accumulator to average. Pan it and the sequence finally varies (7.54 dB). At 3× the gain nearly vanishes (11.31 against 10.85) while the neighbourhood clamp fires on 9,239 pixels a frame: the resolve is too soft for the 3×3 box to admit the history it is being handed.

Which engine is running, and what only one of them does. The line under the controls says whether the spatial panes came off an adapter or off the CPU reference. It is the same picture either way — the kernels are held to the CPU to 2×10−5 on this content, and to 3×10−7 on the kernel gate’s easier one — so the switch buys speed, not a different result. Chained on one encoder the intermediate never returns to the CPU: measured at 64×64 → 128×128 on a software adapter, 4.6–6.9 ms against 28.8–35.1 ms for the CPU, and a third off the two-call form. The temporal pane stays on the CPU whatever the adapter does, because the resolve and the accumulation have no WGSL at all.

RCAS leaves [0,1] and the canvas was hiding it. RCAS_LIMIT keeps the sharpening resolve off the pole of 1/(4·lobe+1); it does not bound the range. The line under the counters reports the real range — on the zone plate it runs to about [−0.23, 1.23] — and everything outside gets clipped when the picture reaches 8 bits. That clipping was always happening; until this page reported it, nothing said so.

Watch the accumulate counters. They are the diagnostic, not decoration. rejected offscreen should be exactly one column — 192 pixels — while panning and zero while static; anything else means the motion vectors disagree with the camera. This page shipped once with that sign inverted, scoring 11.5 dB while panning, and the counter said so before the picture did.

PSNR is the wrong metric for EASU and the right one for the temporal path. EASU is an edge-adaptive filter meant to look sharp, not to minimise L2 against a supersampled truth; it scores below bilinear even on smooth content, which is the metric's verdict and not a fault. Judge it with your eyes; judge the temporal pane with the number.
