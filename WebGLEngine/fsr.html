What each pane is. The scene is a continuous function, so a render is a point sample and the ground truth is an 8×8 area average — the “infinite sample” reference. Bilinear and FSR1 get one unjittered frame. The temporal pane gets a new jittered frame every tick: jitter → render at the low resolution → jitter-aware resolve to display resolution → reprojected accumulation, then RCAS.

What to look for. On the zone plate the temporal pane climbs past every spatial result — it recovers detail the render grid cannot carry. Measured here at 2×, 32 frames, against bilinear’s 14.82 dB: 21.23 dB static, 16.95 dB panning. Panning gains less because each frame resamples the whole history through a bilinear reprojection, and that blur is a running cost a static camera never pays.

The controls are where the mechanism shows its edges. On smooth the temporal pane loses to bilinear (68.54 against 72.13) — there is nothing beyond Nyquist to recover, so accumulation buys nothing and the reprojection’s own resampling still costs. On the pixel checker all four panes sit at 5.50 dB with a static camera: the checker alternates every display pixel, the render grid is half that, and sub-render-pixel jitter never crosses a checker cell — every jittered frame is the same frame, so there is nothing for the accumulator to average. Pan it and the sequence finally varies (7.54 dB). At 3× the gain nearly vanishes (11.31 against 10.85) while the neighbourhood clamp fires on 9,239 pixels a frame: the resolve is too soft for the 3×3 box to admit the history it is being handed.

Which engine is running, and what only one of them does. The line under the controls says whether the spatial panes came off an adapter or off the CPU reference. It is the same picture either way — the kernels are held to the CPU to 2×10−5 on this content, and to 3×10−7 on the kernel gate’s easier one — so the switch buys speed, not a different result. Chained on one encoder the intermediate never returns to the CPU: measured at 64×64 → 128×128 on a software adapter, 4.6–6.9 ms against 28.8–35.1 ms for the CPU, and a third off the two-call form. This paragraph said until v4638 that the temporal pane stays on the CPU whatever the adapter does, “because the resolve and the accumulation have no WGSL at all”. They have had WGSL since v4552 and a caller since v4590, and the script below has called tgpu.resolveAndAccumulate ever since — the comment beside that call was corrected in both of the rounds that changed it and this sentence, thirty lines up and the only version a reader sees, was not. The resolve and the accumulation run chained on one encoder when there is an adapter.

And the sentence that followed it, until v4641, said the dolly was the one pane still CPU-only — “the reject chain has a WGSL twin and no runner on this page yet”. It has one now. render/temporalRejectGPU.mjs runs all three reject passes — disocclusion, history factor, rectify — on one encoder, with neither intermediate crossing back to the CPU, and this page is the first caller it has ever had outside a gate. The line above the panes names it when it is on. The CPU functions stay where they were: they are the reference the device is held to, not a fallback nobody runs.

RCAS leaves [0,1] and the canvas was hiding it. RCAS_LIMIT keeps the sharpening resolve off the pole of 1/(4·lobe+1); it does not bound the range. The line under the counters reports the real range — on the zone plate it runs to about [−0.23, 1.23] — and everything outside gets clipped when the picture reaches 8 bits. That clipping was always happening; until this page reported it, nothing said so.

Watch the accumulate counters. They are the diagnostic, not decoration, and they read the same on the CPU and on an adapter — the WGSL kernel counts them in an atomic buffer and the gate holds the two equal, not merely close, because they are integers. Both readouts end with counted on the device or counted on the CPU, and that tail is there precisely because the two agree: identical integers cannot tell you which engine produced them, so a silent fallback would be invisible without it. rejected offscreen should be exactly one column — 192 pixels — while panning and zero while static; anything else means the motion vectors disagree with the camera. That reading is about the panning camera and does not carry to the dolly, which reads zero: a perspective frustum widens with depth, so at this dolly rate nothing leaves the frame at all. This page shipped once with that sign inverted, scoring 11.5 dB while panning, and the counter said so before the picture did.

The dolly is the one camera that can disocclude anything, and it took both halves to get there. The reject pass in render/temporalReject.mjs asks whether the surface a pixel reprojects onto is the one that was there last frame. For that question to ever have a non-trivial answer two surfaces have to move different screen distances — that is parallax — and all four cells were measured before any of this was written: orthographic over a flat scene (this page’s pan) 0, orthographic over two planes 0, perspective over one plane 0, perspective over two planes 106. The second cell is the one worth reading: an orthographic camera moving parallel to the image plane cannot reveal a disocclusion however much depth the scene has, because the image and the depth buffer translate together. Depth alone is not the condition and perspective alone is not the condition.

So the dolly puts the pattern on two planes, four and eight units out, and walks the camera sideways. The disocclusion line reads 106 genuine — a one-pixel sliver down the slab’s trailing edge, 106 pixels tall — and 0 on every eighth frame, which is not flakiness: the edge advances 0.879 display pixels per frame, so in eight frames it crosses seven pixel boundaries and the depth buffer is nearest-sampled. The threshold is derived from the camera, a quarter of the near/far clip-z gap (0.00626 here), because clip z is nonlinear — the same pair of planes at forty and eighty units apart gives a gap ten times smaller, and a constant tuned here would silently flag nothing there.

What the reject chain is worth, honestly: nothing you can see, and that is the point. 106 pixels is 0.29% of the frame, so switching the chain off moves PSNR from 16.27 dB to 16.26 dB on the zone plate — the metric cannot see it. The counter can: history discarded goes 106 to 0. This is the same argument the accumulate counters make one paragraph up, and the dolly is where it stops being rhetorical.

On an adapter that 106 is now read out of an atomic buffer rather than counted in JavaScript, and getting it there took a second entry point in each of two kernels. The mask the disocclusion pass writes could not have given it: it stores the same 1.0 for a real disocclusion, for an invalid motion vector and for a reprojection that left the frame, so genuine = flagged − noHistory is unrecoverable from the mask by construction — and the runner's own comment used to tell callers to count them from it.

And a sign was wrong again, and again a counter said so first. The dolly jitters its matrix through jitterProjection where the other two cameras jitter the scene sampler, and this page is the first thing in the tree to use jitterProjection and resolveJitterAwareCPU together. They read the same offset in opposite senses — one moves the image, the other moves the sample — and render/jitter.mjs’s convention block fixed the units and the axes and never said which. Paired without negating one, the zone plate scored 11.61 dB and the neighbourhood clamp fired on 34,191 of 36,864 pixels on smooth content; negated, 16.27 dB and 466. Both modules are gated and both are right on their own terms. The clamp counter is what made it visible, three rounds after the same page shipped a motion-vector sign inverted and scored 11.5 dB.

PSNR is the wrong metric for EASU and the right one for the temporal path. EASU is an edge-adaptive filter meant to look sharp, not to minimise L2 against a supersampled truth; it scores below bilinear even on smooth content, which is the metric's verdict and not a fault. Judge it with your eyes; judge the temporal pane with the number.
