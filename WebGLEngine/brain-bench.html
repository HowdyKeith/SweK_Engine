Which half of a brain belongs on a GPU

Both halves are here. This machine decides, and it may not agree with mine.

The question

The brain solves a cost-weighted flow field over a heightmap every tick — "from every cell, which way is downhill toward a goal". There are two solvers: an exact CPU Dijkstra, and a GPU iterative relaxation. GPU-by-default says the parallel one wins.

It does not. On the rig this was written on, the GPU field measured ~50x slower and about 40° less accurate, so the CPU became the default. That is not a story about a bad GPU — it is a story about shape: Dijkstra is sequential and memory-bound, which is the wrong shape for a GPU no matter how good the GPU is. A dense matrix multiply is the right shape, which is why the brain's policy network stayed on the GPU. Two halves, two answers, one measurement each.

But "the rig's grid" and "the rig's GPU" are not universal. A bigger grid, or a stronger discrete card, and the relaxation can win. So the engine stopped guessing: brain/flowfieldAuto.js measures both on your box and picks. This page shows you what it sees.

Why agreement is on this table

A fast wrong field is not a win. The GPU solver relaxes iteratively toward the answer; stop it early and it is quick and wrong. So every row reports the direction each solver chose per cell, compared against exact Dijkstra as ground truth. A speedup column with no accuracy column beside it is a benchmark that cannot fail — and one that always flatters the approximate solver.
