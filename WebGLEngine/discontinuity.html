The repair I recommended twice, on trial

Two rounds recommended the vertex stencil as the only repair for an undetermined gradient that needs no boundary data — and both named the same untested assumption in as many words: it assumes a distant cell is informative. Across a discontinuity it is not, and here it is the widening rather than the boundary condition being measured.
The key is exact. The field is piecewise linear, so on either side the true gradient is a known constant, and any cell whose whole stencil lies on one side must return it exactly. The interface sits on a mesh line, so no cell straddles it and every cell average is exact.

1. Two defects in one number

My first census reported the face stencil 100% wrong on clean cells and the vertex stencil exact — which reads as the discontinuity punishing the narrow stencil, and is nothing of the kind. The 1.000 is the two rank-deficient corner cells returning a zero gradient, a defect the vertex stencil repairs and which has no connection to the interface at all. Excluded from both columns, both stencils are exact away from the jump.

2. The measurement

The widening contaminates exactly twice as many cells and reaches twice as far — 0.33 to 0.67 cells, independent of mesh size. And it halves the peak error, because it averages more data. The two effects pull opposite ways, so a peak-error comparison alone would have called the widening better.
Integrated over the band it is about 20% worse — twice the cells at ~55% of the peak. A peak and a band are not comparable by the peak. This is the first measurement in the arc where the repair I recommended twice is worse than not applying it, on a problem it was never designed for. (The peak growing with refinement is correct: a jump has no finite gradient, so a cell-sized difference over a shrinking cell must diverge as 1/h.)

3. The synthesis

Targeting keeps the narrow contamination and repairs the rank. Same crossing count and integrated damage as the face stencil, with no deficient cells left over. Two rounds ago the lesson was that a rule applied where no repair was needed can only do harm; this is that lesson arriving from the other side. The widening is a rank repair, not a general improvement.
So the recommendation is narrower than it was two rounds ago, which is the point of measuring it: Dirichlet where the data exists; the vertex stencil only at cells whose rank is actually deficient; never as a blanket widening.
