Water waves — a speed you can predict from the depth

Linear surface gravity waves obey ω² = g·k·tanh(k·h). Two limits fall out of it and both are elementary: shallow (kh → 0) gives c = √(gh), the same speed for every wavelength; deep (kh → ∞) gives c = √(g/k), so long waves outrun short ones and the group travels at exactly half the phase speed. Nothing from any grid appears in either.
Checked the tree before writing a line of this. The engine has three height-field waters — water/waterMath.js, render/WaterField.js, world/waveSystem.js — and not one of them contains g, a depth, a dx or a dt. water-selfcheck proves a drop propagates and then decays, and never asks how fast. So the wave speed of the engine's water has never been a number anybody could predict. Below it becomes one.

1. The relation, and the scheme, on the same axes

phase speed against kh, both normalised to their own long-wave value
▬ real water, c/√(gh) ▬ real water, group/phase ▬ the shipped scheme, c(k)/c(0) ▬ the shallow limit, 1.0
Both curves fall with k, and that is the trap. One is physics — short waves really are slower — and the other is a grid error. They are two things wearing one label, and you cannot buy the first with the second: c ∝ k-1/2 is a power law and 2·asin(q·sin(k/2))/k is not one. Fit the scheme's single free constant to the deep-water curve and the best it manages still misses by more than 100% across a modest band.

2. Where the shipped constant sits

Peak amplitude of the worst mode (kx=ky=π) against step count, log scale. waveStep ships s = 2.0, damping = 0.995.
The checkerboard mode gives sin²(ω/2) = s/2, so the 2D stability limit is s ≤ 2 — and the shipped value is exactly 2, on the boundary rather than near it. There the root is defective: it does not explode, it grows linearly, at exactly 2 per step. Set damping to 1.000 and watch it climb forever; put it back and it is held. damping's comment says "lose energy each step" — an artistic knob that turns out to be load-bearing for stability. The file's own line "stable for damping in (0,1)" is true, and true for a different reason than it reads.
