The fix I proposed was wrong

The previous round ended by naming the repair: "a higher-order boundary row — two rows per face, or one per quadrature point — is what would recover the exactness curvature costs." Three measurements say the boundary row was never the limiting factor, and a fourth says what is.

1. The row is already exact at its own quadrature point

A chord's outward normal is the radial direction at the arc midpoint, by symmetry — 4.0e-16 rising only to 7.2e-15 over a sixteenfold refinement. That is round-off, not geometry, and it does not fall with h because there is nothing there to converge. (My first version of this check compared against +r̂ and read exactly 2.000 — two unit vectors pointing opposite ways. On the inner wall of an annulus "outward from the cell" means toward the hole. A worst case of exactly 2.000 between unit vectors is a sign, not an error.)

2. A constant gradient cannot satisfy the row

The exact gradient at the centroid, dotted with the face normal, is O(h) — orders 0.96 / 0.98 / 0.99 once the mesh resolves the circle. The row asks a constant gradient for something a constant gradient does not have, and the first-order convergence measured last round is exactly this residual vanishing.

3. The proposed fix, measured

Two rows per face is worse at every mesh size and converges no faster, because it adds another equation the constant gradient cannot satisfy either. More quadrature points state a true condition more carefully to a model that cannot hear it.

4. What actually moves the category

Order 1.88 against the linear-plus-row's 0.93, and 27× more accurate at the finest mesh — with no boundary row at all. The category moves, but not by the route I proposed. The higher order costs the wider stencil: five unknowns need at least five rows, which a vertex neighbourhood has and a face neighbourhood does not — the same widening trade measured two rounds ago, arriving from a different direction.
Not claimed: that a quadratic is the right choice for a solver. It is ~4× the rows on every cell and has no limiter, so on a discontinuous field it would be the widening under trial again, and worse. What is claimed is narrow: the boundary row was not the limiting factor on a curved wall, and the fix proposed last round makes it worse rather than better.
