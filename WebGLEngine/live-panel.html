📈 Live Panels — the numbers underneath the view

physics/livePanel.js + physics/viewLayout.js. Both were built, gated and reachable from nothing: their only importer was their own selfcheck, so the arithmetic was proven and no page had ever drawn it. This is that page.

The layout — one primary, ordered insets

Click an inset to promote it. The old primary is demoted to an inset, never dropped — that is the property viewLayout-selfcheck plants against, because a promote that discards the previous view loses whatever you were looking at. The layout encodes to a string, so a view is shareable.

Spectrum panel — and it reads the physics rather than decorating it

A chain of 12 masses is driven in one normal mode, sampled, and run through the strict FFT. THE CLAIM IS NOT THAT IT LOOKS LIKE A SPECTRUM. The peak bin is converted back to a frequency and compared against the chain's analytic mode frequency — a number the panel is never told. A decoration would draw bars and land anywhere.

Zeta-line panel — graded against an anchor from outside this engine

|ζ(1/2 + it)| sampled along the critical line. The dips are the nontrivial zeros, and they are compared against the published values — 14.134725, 21.022040, 25.010858 — which nothing in this tree computes or could have fitted. That is what makes this a key rather than a mirror: an internal check could only show the panel agreeing with itself.

READ THE GAP COLUMN AS RESOLUTION, NOT AS ACCURACY. A dip is the index of a local minimum on a sampled curve, and the default sweep takes 190 steps across t = 8…27 — a grid spacing of 0.1. So the dips land on 14.100, 21.000 and 25.000 because those are the grid points, and the gaps below are bounded by the spacing rather than by anything the panel got right or wrong. An observable that is an index cannot see a shift smaller than one cell; quoting 0.035 here as the panel's precision would be reporting the sampling rate wearing a measurement's clothes. Raise steps and the gaps shrink with the grid.

What this page does not claim

It does not claim the panels are fast, and it is not a benchmark. The spectrum runs 8192 samples on page load and blocks for as long as that takes. It also does not claim these are the only two panels the layout can hold: validate() takes the known-panel list as an argument, so adding a third is a registration rather than an edit here.
