Krbn compare — our obj vs Krbn, in lockstep, + lift to 3D

The real emitted geometry (the blobulator's mesh, Krbn's own MeshInput type) drawn two ways: left our shaded 3D, right Krbn's flat line drawing. Drag the wipe anywhere; Play orbits with both sides on the same frame. Lift to 3D ray-casts Krbn's flat points back onto the surface, and Export OBJ writes the whole drawing out as importable 3D polylines — Krbn's line-work as a real object you can orbit in Blender.

Honest scope: the left pane is a real WebGL2 render of the geometry, and it falls back to a canvas-2D render if the GPU or the shader is unavailable (the status line says which is live). Its shader uses the same projection as the Krbn side, so the two stay aligned across the wipe — which was not true until v4045: both this page's project() and its shader used one focal length horizontally and another vertically, stretching everything by exactly W/H (1.64× at 920×560). Because both halves of the wipe repeated the same mistake they agreed with each other and disagreed with Krbn, so the one comparison this page exists to make was the one it could not see. All three now land within 1e-13 px of each other, checked over 300 points by the gate rather than asserted here. The orbit distance is derived from the frustum too, so nothing leaves the frame across a full turn. It is not a byte-for-byte copy of index.html's own shader and lighting — exact parity with the main engine view is a further tuning pass. The Krbn side uses the real perspective projection; the lift and the export use ray–mesh back-projection onto the geometry we already hold. Backed by tools/krbn/krbnCompare.js and tools/krbn/strokeLift.js, both gated.
