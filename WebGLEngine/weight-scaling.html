The optimum converges — but not to one number

The previous round found the boundary row's weight had been a hardcoded 1 for six rounds and that a better value was worth 1.7×. It ended on the right question: does the optimum move with refinement? A fixed constant is defensible only if it doesn't.
And the first answer was my own search grid. A sweep spaced 20.25 reported the argmin moving by exactly one sample at each refinement, twice, then zero — which is what a quantised search reports when the true optimum drifts more slowly than the grid. An argmin over a sweep is an index, and the same law that caught a front measured in cell numbers applies to a search unchanged.

1. The grid was the answer

2. It converges, it does not scale

The increments shrink and neither w*/h nor w*/√h is constant — both grow monotonically, so it isn't a scaling law. It approaches a dimensionless constant near 0.218, and is still 5% short at nt = 768. And the gain is h-independent: choosing the weight is worth the same 1.7× at every resolution, so the cost of leaving it at 1 doesn't shrink as the mesh improves.

3. What it must not depend on, and what it does

Invariant to field amplitude and to overall length scale — bit-identical, as a dimensionless quantity in a linear problem must be. Those are the controls that say the search is measuring the method.
But it is a function of the cell aspect ratio, moving 2.2× across shapes at one refinement — and two entirely different geometries with the same aspect ratio give the same optimum to the last digit. So a constant is defensible at fixed mesh shape and not across shapes, and the shipped default of 1 is outside the whole measured range (0.107 to 0.240) and worse than every point in it. It is left at 1 anyway, so six rounds of numbers still reproduce — the deliverable is that the knob exists, what it tracks, and that the current value is off the end of the range.
