Elliptic integrals — and the pendulum that grades them back

Legendre's F(φ|m), E(φ|m), K(m), E(m) through Carlson's symmetric forms RF and RD (duplication plus a truncated series). The code is the easy half. The reason these are worth having in this tree is the answer key: elliptic integrals carry exact external anchors that no amount of agreeing with myself can manufacture.
Three routes, sharing no code. Carlson duplication; Gauss's arithmetic-geometric mean (no duplication, no series); and composite Simpson straight down the defining integral. Two routes agreeing could be two typos agreeing. Three plus a constant that appears nowhere in the file could not.

1. The exact limits, and why they are not enough on their own

F(φ|0)=E(φ|0)=φ, K(0)=E(0)=π/2, E(φ|1)=sinφ
Not one of those lines can tell the parameter m from the modulus k, because m = k² has fixed points at exactly 0 and 1 — which is where every closed form lives. The literature uses both conventions and calls both of them K. A routine that reads its argument as the modulus passes the whole panel above and is 9% wrong in the interior, which reads like a tolerance problem rather than a wrong function. Two things wearing one label, this time in the textbooks rather than in our code.

2. The interior: three routes, and Legendre's relation

Legendre: E(m)K(1-m) + E(1-m)K(m) - K(m)K(1-m) = π/2 for every m — four values, two parameters, one constant that is not in the file

3. The consumer: a pendulum's exact period

T = 4√(L/g) K(sin²(θ0/2)) is a function of one angle — no grid, no timestep, no machine. So it can grade an integrator rather than be graded by one. RK4 runs in your browser when you press the button.

4. The verification, drawn

physics/pendulumWave.js tunes a row of pendulums so that in one cycle the longest completes 20 swings, the next 21, and so on — released together they fan into a travelling wave and snap back into line at the end. It steps exact simple harmonic motion, so its re-sync is exact for the equation it solves. A real pendulum is not harmonic. At the shipped amplitude of 0.3 rad every one of them runs 0.565% slow, and the row lines up on the green tick below — a time predicted from K alone — rather than at its own nominal 60 s.
t = 0.00 s nominal cycle 60.000 s predicted true cycle s phase spread now: rad
And the picture is a weak instrument, which is the point of saying so. At the moment of re-synchronisation every pendulum sits at its turning point, where dθ/d(phase) = 0. A phase error of 0.1 rad — a visibly late pendulum — moves the drawn angle by amp·ε²/2 ≈ 0.0015 rad, which is nothing. So the drawing is worth having and the number beside it is what grades it. Push the amplitude spread up: the phase readout moves long before your eye does.
