# Round H1 -- the repo's 963-weight networks against the PD autopilot and against their own untrained start, on the tank's compass dropout (REGISTERED 2026-11-04, before any code and before any of its seeds was flown)

## 1. Why
The switchboard (`brain/switchboard.mjs`) can fly any 963-weight network against the PD autopilot on the tank's compass dropout. Its first smoke run (3 ids) said one network beat the autopilot by +0.125 and another tied it; nothing says which of the eight networks in the tree does what, or whether the training is what pays. The fish and fly notes found, again and again, that the structure and the optimizer pay and the connectome does not (`brain/RUNG_4A_CLOSEOUT.md`, `brain/PLAIN_NET_CONTROL_WIP.md` on `claude/sweet-lovelace-8leivh`). This round asks the one question those notes leave for the networks this tree HAS:

> On the tank's compass dropout, does each trained 963-weight network beat the PD autopilot, does it beat the hand-wired weights it was trained from, and is its gain specific to the dark?

It says nothing about the fish pilot (803 weights, not flown by this tank), the stateful fly (not in this tree) or the plain-net control (not in this tree); those arms are not on the board yet and a result here is not about them.

## 2. The arms and the measure
**Arms (ten):** `autopilot` (the PD autopilot); `hand` (the untrained network: `brain/pilotSubPolicy.mjs` handWeights, the weights the training started from); and the eight trained networks in `brain/pilotSubWeights*Wip.json` with 963 weights, by file: **Wip, Actuators, Robust, RobustLong, RobustTail, RobustTail2, RobustWide, RobustNoisy**. The fish file (803 weights) is not flown. **Controls:** every trained network is read against `autopilot` and against `hand`; `hand` against `autopilot`.
**Task:** the switchboard's `dropout`: one-sub tanks, 160 s, the same spawn for every arm (ids sb-<n>), the compass dropout event on; score = 1 - (mean SETTLED dark heading error)/pi (settled = 12 s after a new target). **Lit control:** the same ids with no event; score from all ticks. **Seeds (fresh; the gate and the smoke run used ids 0-3):** ids **2000-2199 (200 ids)**, read once, dark and lit.
**Readings (the series' conventions):** pooled paired differences, BETTER iff mean > 2 se and positive, WORSE iff mean < -2 se, TIES otherwise. **DARK-SPECIFIC** for a network n: (n minus autopilot in the dark) minus (n minus autopilot lit), paired per id, BETTER iff above 2 se.

## 3. The checks
(a failure and the verdicts are NOT WEIGHED): every arm flew 200 ids in both conditions; the ids are the same in every arm; no arm has a score outside (0, 1); the autopilot's dark and lit scores differ on at least one id (the event reaches the flight); the networks are pairwise different on at least one id (ten distinct arms).

## 4. The verdicts and bottom lines
1. `<n> OF 8 NETWORKS BEAT THE AUTOPILOT IN THE DARK (<names>)`, with `; <names> ARE BELOW IT` appended when any is WORSE.
2. `<n> OF 8 NETWORKS BEAT THEIR UNTRAINED START IN THE DARK (<names>)`, with `; <names> ARE BELOW IT` appended.
3. `THE UNTRAINED HAND WEIGHTS <BEAT | TIE | ARE BELOW> THE AUTOPILOT IN THE DARK`.
4. `THE GAIN IS DARK-SPECIFIC IN <n> OF 8 (<names>)`.
5. `LOST SUBS: autopilot <a>, hand <h>, networks <total over all eight> (<max> in the worst)`.
**REPORTED, NOT IN THE VERDICT:** every arm's dark and lit mean; every network's paired difference from the autopilot and from `hand`, dark and lit, with se and the better/worse/same counts; the spread of the eight gains.

## 5. Expectations, recorded BEFORE any seed is flown
At least 5 of 8 networks beat the autopilot in the dark: about 70 percent; all 8: about 25; none: about 10. At least 5 of 8 beat the hand weights: about 60; the hand weights TIE the autopilot (they are the weights the autopilot's own network form starts from): about 55, BEAT it: 15, BELOW: 30. The gain is dark-specific in at least one network: about 35 (the networks were trained to track targets, not to hold through a dropout). The best network by dark gain is RobustNoisy: about 35 (it was trained under sensor noise, which a dark window resembles). No lost sub in any arm: about 85.

## 6. What will be built
`tools/ship/headToHead.mjs` (the arms from a weights map, the paired readings, the dark-specific difference, the checks and the bottom lines; reads nothing from disk), `tools/ship/headToHeadCmd.mjs` (export-free: reads the weight files, flies, prints, writes `brain/rig-results/head-to-head.json`) and `tools/ship/headToHead-selfcheck.mjs` with a mutation pass; then the run (one process, a few minutes) and the result as section 7 here. **NOT IN THIS ROUND:** the fish pilot, the stateful fly, the plain net, the leaky unit, the survey task, the lesion toggle; more than one boat hull; a task other than the compass dropout; any claim about wiring.

## Round H1, RESULT (2026-11-04): ALL EIGHT TRAINED NETWORKS BEAT THE AUTOPILOT, IN THE LIT TANK AS WELL AS IN THE DARK; THE HAND WEIGHTS ARE THE AUTOPILOT; THREE NETWORKS' GAIN IS DARK-SPECIFIC, BY A SMALLER LIT GAIN, NOT A LARGER DARK ONE

200 fresh ids (2000-2199), 160 s, each of ten arms dark and lit, one process (3 min 50 s), read once. The five checks passed. `brain/rig-results/head-to-head.json` holds every run. The result was flown twice with the same numbers: the first print said "the hand weights ARE BELOW the autopilot" for a difference of 1e-10 (two arms that are the same pilot in two number formats, se 0, and a zero-spread list read by its sign); that was a bug in `brain/switchboard.mjs`'s reading, not in the data, and it is fixed (a mean within 1e-9 of 0 is a TIE) with a row and mutants; every other number was identical on the second flight.

**Dark means (autopilot 0.7504):** Wip 0.8365, Actuators 0.8587, Robust 0.8647, RobustLong 0.8513, RobustTail 0.8600, RobustTail2 0.8666, RobustWide 0.8655, RobustNoisy 0.8702. **Dark gain over the autopilot (se about 0.008, better/worse of 200):** Wip +0.0861 (159/41), Actuators +0.1083 (166/34), Robust +0.1142 (171/29), RobustLong +0.1009, RobustTail +0.1096, RobustTail2 +0.1162, RobustWide +0.1150, RobustNoisy **+0.1198 (177/23)**. **Lit gain:** +0.0866 (Wip), +0.1273, +0.1274, +0.1121, +0.1214, +0.0888, +0.0881, +0.0855. **Dark-specific** (dark gain minus lit gain): Wip -0.0005 TIES, Actuators -0.0190 WORSE, Robust -0.0131, RobustLong -0.0112, RobustTail -0.0119 (all TIES), **RobustTail2 +0.0274, RobustWide +0.0270, RobustNoisy +0.0342 BETTER**. Lost subs: none in any arm.
All five registered bottom lines: `8 OF 8 NETWORKS BEAT THE AUTOPILOT IN THE DARK`; `8 OF 8 NETWORKS BEAT THEIR UNTRAINED START IN THE DARK`; `THE UNTRAINED HAND WEIGHTS TIE THE AUTOPILOT IN THE DARK`; `THE GAIN IS DARK-SPECIFIC IN 3 OF 8 (RobustTail2, RobustWide, RobustNoisy)`; `LOST SUBS: autopilot 0, hand 0, networks 0 (0 in the worst)`.

**Against the expectations of section 5 (written first).** At least 5 of 8 beat the autopilot, about 70: held (and all 8, about 25, came out). At least 5 beat the hand weights, about 60: held, but see below. The hand weights TIE the autopilot, about 55: held, exactly. Dark-specific in at least one, about 35: held (three). The best network by dark gain is RobustNoisy, about 35: held (+0.1198, 0.004 above RobustTail2). No lost sub, about 85: held.

**WHAT THIS MEANS and does not.**
* **The hand weights ARE the autopilot.** Their dark and lit scores equal the autopilot's to better than 1e-9 on all 200 ids: the PD autopilot is the hand-wired network's form. So "beats its untrained start" and "beats the autopilot" are ONE finding, not two; the registered line 2 carries no extra evidence, and `network-hand` is, in this tree, the autopilot's own twin.
* **The training pays, and pays everywhere.** Every network gains +0.09 to +0.12 in the dark AND +0.09 to +0.13 in the lit tank, with 159 to 177 of 200 ids better. That is a heading-tracking gain over the PD autopilot (the autopilot is the weak baseline on this tank), not a gain in holding a heading through a dropout.
* **"Dark-specific" is a lit shortfall more than a dark gain.** RobustTail2, RobustWide and RobustNoisy (the three trained under sensor noise or over the wide hull set) gain +0.115 to +0.120 in the dark, the same as the others (+0.109 to +0.114), and gain only +0.086 to +0.089 in the lit tank where the others gain +0.112 to +0.127. What the noise training bought is robustness of the dark reading relative to the lit one, mostly by being worse lit. The one honest statement is that they lose less when the compass goes dark than the networks trained clean do; RobustNoisy leads the dark column by 0.004 over RobustTail2, inside noise.
* **Not shown, said plainly.** Nothing here is about the connectome: every network is the Giant Fiber Circuit stand-in topology, and the plain-net control (a net of the same size that is not wired like the circuit) is not in this tree, so "the wiring pays" is not tested; the notes on the other branch found a plain net keeps 100 to 116 percent of the wiring's gain on the fish tasks, and this round cannot say the same here. One hull, one task, one compass event, ids 2000-2199; a survey-task arm for these networks is not on the board. The fish pilot and the stateful fly are not flown here.
* **What it adds to the series:** the switchboard's first real reading is a clean positive on the flying layer (trained networks beat the PD autopilot by about 0.1 on this tank, every time) set against the mostly negative readings on the connectome questions. The next round with a point is the same table with the plain net as one more arm, which needs `brain/pilotSubPlainNet.mjs` from the other branch.

# Round H3 -- the survey: the twelve trained needs circuits against the rule they started from, the tuned rule at the seat's constants and the twin seat (REGISTERED 2026-11-04, before any code and before any of its seeds was flown)

## 1. Why
The survey seat of this tree is a rule plus a search (the tuned rule at low -0.06 and the twin planner over it; Rounds 8-33). Piece 4 of `brain/NEEDS_AND_LIMITS.md` trained twelve STATEFUL circuit pilots for the same task from the weaker `GUARDED-OBS` start (`brain/rig-results/needsCircuitStageA.rerun-amendment3.jsonl`: the fly's heading loop `cx`, `nostate`, `withheld`, and nine shuffled controls `er`, `layer`, `degree`, three each) and never flew them against the tuned rule or the twin: their own audits read about 0.55-0.66 against 0.86 for `GUARDED-FILTER` there. The switchboard (`brain/switchboard.mjs`) can fly all of them on one task. This round does it, on seeds none of them has seen, and asks the two questions the series has left on this layer:

> (a) Does any trained circuit beat the rule it was trained from, the tuned rule at the seat's constants, or the twin? (b) Does the real wiring (`cx`, `nostate`, `withheld`) beat its shuffles?

It says nothing about the fish or the fly as pilots of a body (that is Round H1's layer), nothing about a circuit trained from a better start (they were all trained from `GUARDED-OBS`), and nothing about a larger search.

## 2. The arms and the measure
**Arms (sixteen):** `rule-obs` (`T.GUARDED_OBS`, the start of every circuit); `rule-r8` (the tuned rule at the Round 8 constants); `rule-low06` (the same at low -0.06, the seat's constants); `twin-low06` (the survey seat, standard profile); and the twelve champions of the file above, rebuilt from their recorded weights by `tools/ship/trainNeedsCircuit.mjs`'s own `policyOf`: `circ-cx`, `circ-nostate`, `circ-withheld`, `circ-er0..2`, `circ-layer0..2`, `circ-degree0..2`. **Task:** the survey task of `brain/needsTask.mjs`, noisy gauge sd 0.10 (the condition the circuits were trained in), 600 s dives, ALL FOUR kinds (plain, deep, far, both), seeds **15001-15060 of each kind (240 dives an arm)**, read once, every arm on the same dives. **Orientation step:** the first-order step ("euler", `rigidBody6dofEuler`), the step every needs-and-limits number was measured under; the box3d line made RK4 the engine default on 2026-11-04 and this round says euler out loud so its numbers stay comparable with Rounds 8-33.
**Readings:** pooled paired differences over all 240 dives (BETTER iff mean > 2 se and positive, WORSE iff mean < -2 se, TIES otherwise; `brain/switchboard.mjs` `readingOf`). Per circuit: against `rule-obs`, against `rule-low06`, against `twin-low06`. **Family comparison (b):** the three real-wiring circuits' mean scores against the nine controls' (each circuit's own 240-dive mean), the difference over its Welch standard error; REAL ABOVE iff the difference is positive and above 2 se, else NOT ABOVE.

## 3. The checks
(a failure and the verdicts are NOT WEIGHED): every arm flew 240 dives; the dives are the same in every arm in the same order; each circuit's rebuilt pilot reproduces its recorded start check (the identity: a pilot with its start weights flies `rule-obs` dive for dive on 4 baseline dives, the check the Piece 4 file records) -- here, the rebuilt pilot's weight count equals the record's; the four rules and the twin are pairwise different on at least one dive; the survey seat, rule-low06 and rule-r8 reproduce their Round 33 selection arms' scores on three dives each (the reproduction of the rule and seat numbers under euler).

## 4. The bottom lines
1. `<n> OF 12 CIRCUITS BEAT THEIR START (RULE-OBS) (<names>)`, with `; <names> ARE BELOW IT` appended.
2. `<n> OF 12 CIRCUITS BEAT THE TUNED RULE AT THE SEAT'S CONSTANTS (<names>)`, with `; <names> ARE BELOW IT` appended.
3. `THE BEST CIRCUIT (<name>, <mean>) <BEATS | TIES | IS BELOW> THE TWIN SEAT (<difference>)`.
4. `THE REAL WIRINGS AVERAGE <m1> AND THE SHUFFLED CONTROLS <m2>: <REAL ABOVE | NOT ABOVE> (difference <d>, 2 se <e>)`.
5. `HULL: crushed or stranded dives: rule-low06 <a>, twin <b>, circuits <total> (<max> in the worst)`.
**REPORTED, NOT IN THE VERDICT:** every arm's mean and its bad count; the per-kind means of the four rules, the twin and the best circuit; every circuit's three paired differences with se and counts.
