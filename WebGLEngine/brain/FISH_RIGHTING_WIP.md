## 2. The question, and what a result would and would not mean
**THE QUESTION.** After an instant change of the pitch rate of 0.3 rad/s (three times the kick the circuits were fitted to) on a steady dive, does each family of trained pilots bring the pitch back to where it would have been, faster than the PD autopilot does, and does the plain-net family do it as fast as the fish-wired family?
**WHAT A RESULT WOULD MEAN:** these pilots, this kick, these dives, this measure of recovery. **WHAT IT WOULD NOT MEAN:** that a fish rights itself; that the wiring did it (the plain nets are the control); anything about roll or yaw (the kick is in pitch only); anything in the tank page (no event there yet); that a kick of another size or a trim would read the same.

## 3. The task and the measure (fixed here, before any code)
**Dives.** 48 dives of kind `nominal`, scenario seeds **190001 to 190048** (no file in the tree uses them, checked with a whole-word search of the .mjs, .md, .js, .json and .jsonl files; the first draft of this section said 94001 to 94048, which the box3d joust notes use for another task, found before any code was written and any kick flown, and replaced here), each 40 s. **Kick.** At t = 20 s (tick 600) the pitch rate `w[1]` is changed by `sign x 0.3` rad/s, `sign` = +1 (nose down, the vessel's convention) and -1 (nose up), each dive flown with both signs: **96 kicks a pilot.** Each kick flight is paired with the SAME pilot's flight of the same dive with no kick. **d(t)** = pitch with the kick minus pitch without it, per tick, from the kick to 20 s after it. **AREA** = the integral of |d| over those 20 s in rad s (**the measure**); PEAK = max |d|; SETTLE = the first time after which |d| stays within 0.02 rad until the end of the window (20 s if never). A flight that ends early (crushed, grounded, broached) takes the penalty **AREA = 10 rad s** (0.5 rad over the whole window) and is counted and printed. SETTLE and PEAK are printed, not in the verdict.
**Arms.** AUTOPILOT; FISH803 (one pilot); REAL = 8 circuits (`P/B/qns:0..3`, `P/whole/qns:0..3`); PLAIN = 16 nets (`P/B/mn:0..7`, `P/whole/mn:0..7`). The records' own champions are used; none is chosen among by any score from this round (the families are taken whole).
**Reading.** A pilot's GAIN is the autopilot's mean AREA minus the pilot's, over the 96 kicks. A family's gain is the mean over its pilots and its se is the sd across pilots over the square root of their number (REAL 8, PLAIN 16: replicates, as the series learned from H7); FISH803 is one pilot, its se is that of the 96 paired differences. **FASTER** iff the gain is positive, above 2 se, AND at least 10 percent of the autopilot's AREA (a floor against the arithmetic of a tiny se); **SLOWER** iff the mirror; else **TIES**. REAL against PLAIN: the difference of the two families' mean AREA with the Welch se, FISH-WIRED FASTER / TIES / PLAIN FASTER by the same 2 se and 10 percent.
**BOTTOM LINE, generated by the rule written here, the first that applies:**
1. any flight lost in any arm -> `A FLIGHT WAS LOST: THE AREAS ARE READ WITH THE PENALTY` (and the rest is read with that in front of it).
2. REAL FASTER and PLAIN FASTER and REAL against PLAIN TIES -> `NETS RIGHT FASTER, AND THE FISH WIRING IS NOT WHAT DID IT`.
3. REAL FASTER and PLAIN not FASTER -> `ONLY THE FISH-WIRED CIRCUITS RIGHT FASTER`.
4. REAL not FASTER and PLAIN FASTER -> `THE PLAIN NETS RIGHT FASTER AND THE FISH CIRCUITS DO NOT`.
5. otherwise -> `NEITHER FAMILY RIGHTS FASTER THAN THE AUTOPILOT`.

**AMENDMENT A (2026-11-07, while writing `brain/subRighting.mjs`, BEFORE any kick was flown and before the dives were built):** rule 2 as first written required REAL against PLAIN to TIE, so two families that were BOTH faster than the autopilot but differed from each other fell through to rule 5 and would have been reported as `NEITHER FAMILY RIGHTS FASTER`, which is false. Rule 2 is now: REAL FASTER and PLAIN FASTER -> `NETS RIGHT FASTER, AND THE FISH WIRING IS NOT WHAT DID IT` if REAL against PLAIN TIES, `BOTH FAMILIES RIGHT FASTER, AND THE FISH-WIRED CIRCUITS BY MORE` if the fish-wired are faster than the plain by the test, `BOTH FAMILIES RIGHT FASTER, AND THE PLAIN NETS BY MORE` if the plain are faster. Rules 1, 3, 4 and 5 are unchanged; the expectation of rule 2 (about 22) covers its first sentence, and the other two split about 3 percent between them (taken from rule 5's 55).

## 4. Checks, run first; a failure is the first line of the report and the readings are NOT WEIGHED
(1) The runner with no kick reproduces `subTask.fly` (score, status, fingerprint) for the autopilot on the first four dives. (2) Each rebuilt pilot flies the 4b-P held-out dives to the score its record kept for its champion search (to 1e-4: the records keep the weights to six decimals), so the pilot flown here is the trained one. (3) Every arm flew the same 96 kicks in the same order. (4) The kick moves the pitch rate by exactly 0.3 at tick 600 in the autopilot's flight, in the sign asked (a check on the runner, not a reading).

## 5. Expectations, written before any kick is flown
The circuits and the nets were fitted to a kick of 0.1 and a trim, on a score that hardly sees the kick; the autopilot is already a stiff PD law on pitch (about 57 kN m per rad of righting). So: **REAL FASTER about 35 percent; PLAIN FASTER about 35; FISH803 FASTER about 12** (it was fitted to nothing about kicks); **any family SLOWER about 15**; REAL against PLAIN TIES about 75. Bottom lines: **2 about 22; 3 about 8; 4 about 8; 5 about 55; 1 about 7.** The most likely single outcome is that nothing is faster, in which case the demo's claim ("the pilot rights itself faster than the autopilot") would be false for these pilots, and that is a finding.

**AMENDMENT B (2026-11-07, made on DEV seeds 1 to 3 flying the AUTOPILOT ALONE, before any trained pilot was flown on a kick and before the registered dives were built or flown):** the first draft's AREA was the integral of |d| over 20 s.
