## 2. The question, its units and what is a family-level claim
**Q: does a plain-net search that passes the pool filter beat the rule on far-and-both dives, on average, as a family of searches?** The unit is the SEARCH (an independent training run); the filter is the one every earlier round used (ELIGIBLE: crushed no more pool dives than the start's 0), decided from the training ledger before any dive of a new block is flown. **Statement the round can support: "a search of this recipe that passes the pool filter leads the rule by about X on far-and-both dives." It cannot support a statement about searches that fail the filter (they are not flown), about plain or deep dives, about the circuits (no new circuit search is trained), or about the hull's cost (reported, not part of either verdict).**

## 4. The questions
* **CHECKS, run first (a failure is the first line of the report and the verdicts are NOT WEIGHED).** SPECS (the two ledgers hold exactly `plain:4`-`19` and `plain-priced:4`-`19`, once each, with the seed of index k equal to 96520 + k, one recipe in its iterations, sigma, maxK and prune budget as the old ledgers', the start numbers of every record equal to the old ledgers' start); ELIGIBLE (a file for every new search that is eligible by the ledger and no other, and the old eligible list equal to the sixteen named above); ARMS (each new file holds the rule and its search once each; the old file holds the rule and the sixteen once each); WEIGHTS (every digest is the ledger's); BLOCKS (every file flew its registered range and kinds); KEYS (both arms of a file flew the same dives in the same order); SCORES (numbers in [0, 1], strains numbers or null); DISJOINT (no dive in two files).
* **Q1 (VERDICT 1: THE FAMILY).** The eligible new searches of both families together, the lead of each over the rule on its own 1,200 dives, **the EXACT sign-flip test of the sum of the leads** (every assignment of signs to the absolute leads, 2^n of them, counted exactly; p+ = the share of assignments whose sum is at least the observed sum, p- the share at most it; |lead| below 1e-12 counts as 0): **`THE PLAIN FAMILIES LEAD THE RULE`** iff p+ is at most 0.05; **`THE PLAIN FAMILIES ARE BELOW THE RULE`** iff p- is at most 0.05; otherwise **`THE PLAIN FAMILIES CANNOT BE TOLD APART FROM THE RULE`**. The test assumes the searches' leads are independent and symmetric about 0 under the null; independence is what the private blocks buy; symmetry is an assumption, said here.
* **Q2 (VERDICT 2: THE FOUR OLD ONES, FRESH DIVES).** On the common block, the four old plain-family eligible searches' per-dive paired differences over the rule averaged over the four, the mean and its se over the 2,400 dives (sample sd / sqrt(2,400)): z = mean / se. **`THE FOUR OLD PLAIN-FAMILY SEARCHES STILL LEAD THE RULE`** iff z is at least 1.645; **`THE FOUR OLD PLAIN-FAMILY SEARCHES TRAIL THE RULE`** iff z is at most -1.645; otherwise **`THE FOUR OLD PLAIN-FAMILY SEARCHES CANNOT BE TOLD APART FROM THE RULE ON THE FRESH BLOCK`**. It is a statement about these four searches, not about the family.
* **Q3 (REPORTED): the new searches by family** (`plain`, `plain-priced`): trained 16, eligible n, how many leads are above 0, the mean lead, the standard deviation across the eligible searches, t = mean / (sd / sqrt(n)), the exact sign-flip p both ways for that family alone, the median; and the difference of the two family means (priced minus unpriced) with Welch's t.
* **Q4 (REPORTED): what the pool filter does:** for each new family the searches that are not eligible, each with its pool crash count and its training-line held-out lead (not flown), and the mean training-line held-out lead of the eligible and of the ineligible.
* **Q5 (REPORTED): the old sixteen on the common block:** each eligible old search's lead (se) and, by family, the mean lead over its eligible searches and how many are above 0; the four plain-family ones individually; the mean over the twelve circuit-family searches and the difference (plain-family four minus circuit twelve) with Welch's t. **They share dives, so the se understates the spread and no test is given.**
* **Q6 (REPORTED): the champion rule on the new searches:** the champion of each new family among its eligible searches by training-line held-out (ties to the lower k), its lead, and the rank of that lead among the family's eligible leads.
* **Q7 (REPORTED): the hull:** for each eligible new search the dives above strain 0.92 and 0.95 of its 1,200, its largest strain, crushed and stranded, with the rule's on the same block; the family totals; and the same for the four old ones on the common block.

**WHAT A RESULT WOULD AND WOULD NOT MEAN.** LEAD: a plain-net search of this recipe and start that passes the pool filter beats the rule on far-and-both dives on average, small as the lead is; it does not say any one search will (Q3 gives the spread), nor that the lead is worth its hull cost (Q7), nor anything about circuits, about plain or deep dives, or about other recipes. CANNOT BE TOLD APART: round 9's pattern across the four old ones was luck or too small for sixteen-odd searches to see; it does not say the leads are zero. BELOW: a finding about the family and the old pattern's selection. **Two verdicts and five reported readings: any reported reading that looks striking is one of about forty numbers.**
