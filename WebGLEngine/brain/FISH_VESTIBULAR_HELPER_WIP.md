### What Keith decided, and what was checked of the research he cited
**Decided (Keith, 2026-10-24):** the 84 afferents, the Mauthner/commissural network, all 47 central neurons, organized into a two-pathway architecture. The 84 and the 47 are exactly what "every afferent-to-neuron pair with at least one synapse" gives (`tools/fishVestibularFacts.py`).
**Relayed with it, then read against the papers (all four are open access and were fetched from the PubMed Central Open Access Subset; the texts were read for the sentences below, not end to end):**
* *"Bianco et al. (2012): tangential-nucleus neurons act as a broadband accelerometer essential for utricle-dependent gaze stabilization."* **Supported, with one distinction.** The abstract (Curr Biol 22:1285-95) says ablations identified a class of central vestibular neurons in the tangential nucleus that are "essential for the utricle-dependent VOR" (a finding),
  and "We propose that tangential neurons function as a broadband inertial accelerometer" (the authors' proposal, not a result). It also says they "project contralaterally to extraocular motoneurons, and in addition, to multiple sites within the reticulospinal complex", "processing utricular acceleration signals to control the activity of extraocular and postural neurons". That last sentence is the only output description for a class in these tables.
* *"Schoppik lab: an architectural asymmetry favoring nose-up compensation."* **Supported, and narrower than the wording.** Schoppik et al. 2017 (J Neurosci 37:11353-65): a genetically defined central vestibular population in rhombomeres 5-7 "project[s] preferentially to motoneurons that move the eyes downward"; stimulating it gives only downward eye rotations;
  ablating it abolishes eye rotation after both nose-up and nose-down tilts; and a model finds that asymmetric projection "could enhance the capacity to represent nose-up stimuli without compromising gaze stabilization". The paper itself labels the superior-oblique / inferior-rectus motoneurons, the pool that rotates the eyes down, "nose-up". So the asymmetry is in a subset's projections to eye motoneurons and in what the model says about representation; it is not in the afferent-to-central tables here, which hold no motoneurons.
  The 2023 birthdate paper (Curr Biol 33:1265-81) adds that tangential projection neurons divide into nose-up and nose-down subtypes by birthdate (only its abstract and a few sentences were read).
* *"Coordinated trunk-pectoral fin synergies during vertical orientation."* **Supported; behavioural, not circuit.** Ehrlich & Schoppik 2019 (eLife 8:e45839, CC BY): larvae "used their pectoral fins and bodies synergistically during upwards swims"; the "fin-body synergy was absent in vestibular mutants" (otolith-deficient `otogelin`) and "systematically altered following cerebellar lesions". Nothing in the tables here connects a central cell to a fin.
* **Not in your summary but in the data's own paper (Liu 2022), and it is the two-pathway architecture:** "Early-born pathways carrying phasic information preferentially excite fast escape circuits, whereas later-born pathways carrying tonic signals excite slower postural and oculomotor circuits." The raw tables reproduce its Fig. 6c claim: 68% of the synapses onto the escape cells come from myelinated afferents, against 39% onto the posture and gaze cells.

### The circuit (the extraction rule, fixed now; `tools/fishVestibularFacts.py` re-derives every number)
* **Cells.** 84 input cells (the afferents with at least one synapse onto a central neuron; the other 22 afferents reach none of the 47 and are left out and counted) and 47 central cells. 131 in all. Right side only; every name ends `_R`.
* **Edges.** Every (afferent, central neuron) pair with at least one synapse: **332 edges, 580 synapses**. Weight = the synapse count. No floor above 1, because the 84 and the 47 are the floor-1 numbers (a floor of 2 would leave 143 edges, 57 afferents, 44 neurons; a floor of 3, 64 edges, 37 afferents, 27 neurons -- reported, not used). There is no hair-cell layer in the circuit and no central-to-central edge, because neither table has one to give.
* **The two pathways.** **E (escape):** the Mauthner cell and the 4 vestibular commissural cells, 5 cells, 31 edges, 81 synapses. **B (balance):** the 12 superior vestibular nucleus, 11 tangential-nucleus and 19 vestibulospinal cells, 42 cells, 301 edges, 499 synapses. The split is the paper's (escape against postural-and-oculomotor), fixed from the cell classes and not fitted to anything.
  The pathways share input: of the 84 afferents, 8 reach only E, 59 only B and 17 both. Kinetic class of an input cell: **phasic** if myelinated (16, exactly `Ear_AnteriorMacula_R_01..16`), **tonic** otherwise (68). The paper INFERS phasic and tonic firing from anatomy (striolar and extrastriolar hair cells, myelination); no firing was recorded here.
* **Tilt tuning (data, not a choice).** Each afferent and each central neuron has the workbooks' own tuning angle in radians: 0 = maximally sensitive to rightward (ipsilateral) tilt, pi/2 = maximally sensitive to rostral tilt (the Source Data's notes). Of the 84 afferents 51 lean rostral and 33 caudal, 74 ipsilateral and 10 contralateral. The Mauthner cell's tuning is ipsilateral and slightly rostral (0.43 rad), the commissural cells' contralateral and caudal (3 of 4), as the paper says.
  **Rostral tilt is read here as nose-down** (the gravity vector moves toward the head's rostral end): the paper's rostral-tilt-tuned tangential axons are "presumed to activate the eyes-up pathway", and eyes-up compensates a nose-down tilt. That is an inference from the paper's own mapping, to be held by a gate row (a nose-down body must drive rostral-tuned afferents more than caudal-tuned ones).
* **Signs.** All 332 edges are excitatory (afferents "excite a variety of central targets"). No central-to-central edge exists, so no central cell's own sign enters the circuit, and the readout is dense and unconstrained as in rung 3. **This removes the signs blocker named earlier: it does not need the Bianco or Schoppik papers for the wiring.** What the papers do give, for any later sign-constrained readout: superior vestibular nucleus inhibitory and tangential excitatory (Liu 2022, citing earlier work), the tangential-type projection population "excitatory" (Schoppik 2017); vestibulospinal and Mauthner not stated in these texts; commissural speculative either way.

### The pilot (rung 3's method; written against the fish chat's note, whose code is not on `main` and was not read)
Per the rung 3 note: output = the autopilot's laws + a residual from the circuit; the 14 features; 4 controls read ONLY the output cells, dense, trainable, starting at ZERO, plus 4 biases; each edge's weight is a trained magnitude starting at 0.3 x count / mean count; the same sparse (1+1)-ES recipe, eligibility and champion rule; a circuit with no eligible champion scores as the autopilot. Two variants, each judged on its own by the rule below:
* **4b-D (dense input, rung-3-comparable).** The 14 features reach the 84 afferents densely and trainably. Trainable: 14 x 84 + 332 + 131 biases + 4 x 47 + 4 = **1,831** weights (arithmetic, to be checked against the code). The data's role is the mask and the counts only.
* **4b-T (tuning-derived input, the variant that uses what the data adds).** NO dense input layer. Each afferent i is driven by the tilt features projected on its own tuning angle: x_i = sin(theta_i) . P_i + cos(theta_i) . R_i, where P_i and R_i are the nose-down pitch and the right-roll signals in the afferent's kinetic form: **phasic** for myelinated afferents (the pitch- and roll-RATE features, the derivative of tilt, which a stateless pilot has) and **tonic** for the rest (the attitude features). Every input is scaled to unit RMS over the training dives, a rule with no tuned constant. Trainable: 332 + 131 + 188 + 4 = **655** weights. The sub's own feature names and sign conventions are to be fixed against `physics/mechanics/subModel6dof.mjs` and `brain/subTask.mjs`, by a gate row, before any training.
* **Readout.** All 47 central cells (Keith's choice). REPORTED, NOT IN THE VERDICT: the score with E's cells cut from the readout, with B's cut, and the share of the readout weight on each, so the two-pathway reading has a number to rest on.

### The task, and its validity rule (the parameters are NOT fixed yet)
A pitch-hold dive with TWO disturbance kinds, so the pathways have something to differ on: a **steady tilt** (a trim offset held for the dive, the tonic part) and **impulsive pitch kicks** (a short pitch-rate disturbance, the phasic part), both added to the standard dives, in seeded windows, as `tools/subDisturb.mjs` and the rung 4a dropout do. The rule, fixed now as rung 4a's was: **the disturbance must cost the autopilot alone at least 0.05 of mean dive score on the 40 training dives**; a ladder of magnitudes is declared in this note BEFORE the first measurement, the smallest rung that clears the floor is THE TASK, and its measurements are written here. A task the autopilot passes unharmed cannot tell circuits apart.

### Controls and the verdict (rung 3's rule, unchanged except for the controls that a two-layer circuit makes meaningless)
* **Controls, all with the same cells, input and output cells, edge count and counts:** **ER** (the 332 edges redrawn uniformly between afferents and central cells, with their counts); **CLASS** (replaces rung 3's LAYER, which would equal ER in a two-layer circuit): every edge keeps its source's kinetic class (phasic or tonic) and its target's pathway (E or B) and gets random endpoints inside those blocks, so the two-pathway BLOCK structure is kept and only the wiring inside it is destroyed -- this is the control that decides whether the architecture is more than the split;
  **DEGREE** (target swaps: every cell keeps its in- and out-degree, every edge its source and count). REPORTED, NOT IN THE VERDICT: **COUNT** (counts permuted across the edges) and, for 4b-T only, **TUNING** (tuning angles and kinetic classes permuted among the afferents with the wiring as it is: do the data's tilt directions matter?). SIGN does not exist here (no signed cell).
* **Fresh audits** at a block of seeds nothing in the tree has touched, chosen and written here when the task is fixed (the fish chat has spent 7000.. and plans 20000.. for its rung 3; nothing was run here). Each circuit's champion reads them once.
* **Verdict, per variant:** the circuit BEATS a family on an audit iff F - m > max(0.02, 2 s). **PASS** iff it beats ER, CLASS and DEGREE on all three audits. **FAIL** (the wiring is ruled out at this rung as a pilot of this kind) iff, against the family with the highest mean on that audit, it does not win on at least two of three. Else: in between, next step named. Reach (the input-to-output pairs the closure connects) is printed for every circuit with the rank correlation against score across the controls, as in rung 3: if score rises with reach, a pass is reported as confounded by reach.
* **Staged** as rung 3: stage A the circuit, 10 ER, 10 CLASS, 10 DEGREE, 4 COUNT (and 4 TUNING for 4b-T); stage B (only if stage A reads as a pass) 10 more of each of the three verdict families.
* **Expectation, recorded now so it can be held against the result: 4b-D fails, and 4b-T most likely does too.** A stateless two-layer map with a dense trained readout has nowhere to need the wiring (rung 3's lesson: most of the gain sits in the dense layers every circuit has), and the autopilot already holds pitch by PD law. 4b-T is the only variant with a route to a positive result, because the tuning-derived input gives the real wiring information the shuffled controls do not have; the CLASS and TUNING controls will say whether a win is the wiring, the encoding, or only the block split. A null here is a statement about this recipe on this interface as much as about the fish.

## ADDENDUM 1, 2026-10-24, BEFORE ANY PILOT IS TRAINED: rung 4b is two tasks, one for each pathway
**Keith's instruction: "Split the vestibular rung into two tasks."** Read as the two pathways of the registration above, each given the task it is for. Everything registered above stands except what is named here. Where this note had to make a choice of its own to keep the split well-posed it is marked **(note's choice)**, so that Keith can reverse it before the first training run. The per-arm counts come from `tools/fishVestibularFacts.py` (its new "three arms" block).

**The two tasks**
* **4b-P, pitch-hold (the balance pathway).** The task of the registration, unchanged: a steady trim offset and impulsive pitch-rate kicks on the standard dives, the validity rule (the disturbance must cost the autopilot alone at least 0.05 of mean dive score on the 40 training dives), and the smallest ladder rung that clears it, the ladder declared before measuring. Aligned circuit **B**: 76 inputs (13 phasic, 63 tonic), 42 central cells (12 superior vestibular nucleus, 11 tangential, 19 vestibulospinal), 301 edges, 499 synapses.
* **4b-E, lateral hazard-escape reflex (the escape pathway); the fish ladder's rung 4, "a looming hazard from one side ... no weapons, no guidance".** Aligned circuit **E**: 25 inputs (14 phasic, 11 tonic), 5 central cells (the Mauthner cell and the 4 commissural), 31 edges, 81 synapses. Fixed now:
  * the hazard is a body that does not home: launched from the left or the right (seeded, even odds) on a straight line at constant speed, aimed once, at launch, at where the hull would be after the lead time if it held its velocity; nothing steers it afterward. Name for it: an **unguided straight-runner, such as a drifting log or an unguided torpedo**; a homing or guided weapon is excluded by the ladder's "no guidance", and a depth charge is a different task (vertical, and its shock arrives with its cue, so there is nothing to dodge);
  * the pilot's only warning is a cue at launch, on the hazard's side: a short sway-velocity step and a roll-rate kick, standing for the pressure pulse that runs ahead of a moving body in water; the pilot is not told the side, the lead time or the speed;
  * a hit (the hull's centre inside the hazard's radius at any tick) ends the dive and is charged as `brain/subTask.mjs` charges every dive that ends early: the worst tick, 7.5, for every tick not flown, so being hit is never a way to stop being graded;
  * a run is a standard nominal dive in still water with one launch at a seeded time in its middle third.
* **Not fixed, and the rule that fixes it (note's choice for the second clause).** The radius, speed, lead time and cue size are a ladder, declared in a second addendum before the first measurement and climbed one rung at a time. The task is the smallest rung that clears BOTH, measured on the 40 training dives: **(1) cost**, the hazard costs the autopilot alone at least 0.05 of mean dive score; **(2) reachable**, the autopilot plus a hand-written reflex (full yaw away from the cue's side and full thrust for 3 s) wins back at least half of that cost. A hazard that passes (1) and not (2) cannot be dodged on this vessel and tells no circuit from another. If no rung passes both, 4b-E is **VOID** and reported as such with its ladder; nothing is retuned afterward.
* **The interface rule.** The cue must reach the pilot through features the pilot has. Which of its 14 features carry the lateral channel (bank, roll rate, body-frame sway) is fixed by a gate row against the pilot's code before training, as the nose-down row is for pitch. If the interface has no lateral feature, 4b-E is VOID, not patched.

**The sign of the lateral cue (to be held by that gate row).** For an otolith, accelerating to the left is the same as tilting to the right (what it feels is acceleration minus gravity). A hazard on the right pushes the hull to the left and so drives the right circuit's ipsilateral-tuned afferents (angle 0) as a right-ear-down tilt does; the Mauthner cell on the hazard's side is the one it excites, as in the animal. That is an inference from the equivalence plus the workbooks' angle convention; the roll-rate kick is a canal signal and is the phasic stand-in exactly as the pitch-rate is in the registration.

**One right circuit, mirrored (note's choice).** The tables are the right side only. Every pilot of 4b is a mirrored pair of its circuit: the left copy has the same weights (tied, so no weight is added) with the lateral (cosine) input terms sign-flipped, and the two outputs are summed with yaw odd under the mirror and pitch, thrust and ballast even. A hazard on the left is then answered by the left copy as one on the right is by the right. Without it a left hazard would reach the right circuit only through its contralateral-tuned inputs, 8 of E's 25 and 3 of B's 76, and the test would be of the weakest part of the data.

**Arms (note's choice): three circuits on each task.** *Aligned* (B on 4b-P, E on 4b-E), *whole* (Keith's registered circuit: all 47 cells, 84 inputs, 332 edges, 580 synapses), and *cross* (the other pathway's circuit on the task). Trainable weights, T / D by the registration's arithmetic: B 591 / 1,655; E 85 / 435; whole 655 / 1,831 (to be checked against the code, as registered).

**Variants (note's choice).** 4b-T is the verdict variant of every arm on both tasks. 4b-D runs only on the whole arm of 4b-P, as rung 3's comparable, and is reported, not judged. The registration expected 4b-D to fail and gave it no route to a result; with four verdict cells below, running it on all of them would double the compute for a variant expected to fail.

**Controls.** ER, DEGREE, and the reported COUNT and TUNING, as registered. **CLASS (note's choice)** keeps each edge's source kinetic class AND its target's cell class (Mauthner, commissural, superior vestibular nucleus, tangential, vestibulospinal). The registration's "target's pathway" keeps nothing about the targets in a one-pathway arm; the cell class implies the pathway, so on the whole arm this is no weaker and is stricter.

**Verdict.** Rung 3's rule as registered, per verdict cell; there are four: (4b-P, B), (4b-P, whole), (4b-E, E), (4b-E, whole). One fresh audit block per task, chosen and written when that task is fixed (none is chosen or spent here). Reported with every result: how many of the four cells passed (a pass in one of four is "one of four", not "the wiring works"), and reach as registered. **The two-pathway claim (note's choice) holds iff** the aligned arm PASSES on its own task on both tasks AND each aligned arm beats its cross arm on that task by max(0.02, 2 sd) on at least two of three audits (a double dissociation). One pathway passing is that pathway's finding only; the architecture is not claimed from it.

**Expectation, recorded now.** *4b-P*: as registered; B fails the family rule, because the autopilot already holds pitch and the dense layers take what gain there is. *4b-E*: the best-posed of the four cells for a positive result, because E has 85 trainable weights and the dense layers cannot absorb the gain. But a reflex is "sign of the lateral cue, yaw away", and any wiring that carries a lateral input to the readout can learn it; I expect ER and CLASS to learn it too, which reads as a pass confounded by reach or as "in between", and E passes cleanly only if the real wiring's phasic-heavy input (14 of 25 inputs, against 13 of 76 in B) matters. I do not expect the double dissociation: both pathways carry pitch and lateral tuning (B 48 rostral-leaning, 28 caudal; E 16 and 9; B 73 ipsilateral, 3 contralateral; E 17 and 8), so each can do the other's task through a trained readout. A task this recipe cannot tell apart is a finding about the recipe.

## ADDENDUM 2, 2026-10-24, BEFORE ANY MEASUREMENT: the interface check, and the ladders

**Four things the gate found that are worth saying before any run:**
1. **The two leaks compound.** A central cell takes the afferent's NEW leaky activity and leaks itself, so after a one-tick pulse the cell's response BUILDS for a while before it fades (on the toy, with lambda 0.9, the thrust residual rises over the next two ticks). Addendum 5's picture of "a time constant of about 100 ticks" is the time constant of each cell alone; the pair is a second-order response with a later peak. Registered as built; a reader of a trained result should expect a delayed, smoothed reflex.
2. **The mirror cancels a yaw bias and doubles the other biases.** The readout bias on yaw is odd under the mirror, so the two copies subtract it to zero; a pitch, thrust or ballast bias is counted twice. A trained circuit therefore has no yaw offset to learn, which is the mirror doing what Addendum 1 said it would.
3. **The end-to-end sign holds on real hazard dives, the magnitude does not mirror.** On rung 0, one dive seed, a hazard from the right and one from the left change the residual yaw (against the same dive with no hazard) in opposite directions, as the pair requires, but by -0.115 and +0.246 with hand-set readout weights: the autopilot's own motion is a different background for the two copies, and the relu does not subtract it out. Not a defect; it is a reason to read a trained pilot's left and right dodges separately.
4. **A worked answer for Addendum 5's NOT CHECKED:** with lambda 0.99 and a readout that adds every central cell to the yaw, the cue's effect on the residual yaw is still 0.068 of an early 0.115 (59 percent) three seconds after the launch; with NO STATE it is a small fraction of its early size. So a 3 s hold is expressible; whether a trained readout finds the reflex is the training's question.
