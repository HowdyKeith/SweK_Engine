## The model (numbers are for a TANK-SCALE demo, said plainly: a real boat's battery is two orders of magnitude bigger)

**Energy.** `E` in joules, capacity by class. Electrical power = hotel load + hydrodynamic power over an efficiency: `P = 200 W + |T x u| / 0.5`, `T` the thrust actually applied (thrustMax x command x submerged fraction), `u` the forward speed. Power rises with the CUBE of speed in steady flight (drag ~ u^2, power ~ T u). `E` never goes below 0 or above capacity; at 0 the screw is dead (the thrust command is forced to 0), the planes and the ballast tank still work (the pump and the planes are not charged for: said, not modelled).
Refill (charge power `chargeW` = 6 kW): only while the centre is within `refillDepth` of the surface -- battery 0.7 m (the surfacing pilot floats the Explorer's centre at 0.56 m), snorkel 4 m (EXPOSED there: the reading says so, since a snorkelling boat is the noisy one). Air-independent hulls have 4 x the capacity and NEVER refill; `unlimited` neither drains nor needs to.

**Pressure and damage.** Pressure = rho g depth (reading). `collapseDepth = crushDepth x (1 - 0.5 x damage)`; STRAIN = depth / collapseDepth. Below `yieldFrac` (0.8) a hull takes no damage from depth; above it damage creeps up at `0.002 x (strain - 0.8)/0.2` per second (a hull held near its limit wears out: it takes ~8 minutes at strain 1.0). `injure(amount)` adds damage at once (a grounding, a collision: the CALLER says how much; nothing here decides what hurts), clamped to [0, 1]; a NaN or a negative amount does nothing. Leak = `0.01 x damage x strain^2` of the weight per second, accumulating as flooding up to 15% (`weightError` of the core, which the flood task already uses: flooded water is weight). Collapse (`status: "crushed"`) when depth exceeds the collapse depth -- so a damaged hull is crushed at a depth an undamaged one survives; the core's own 100 m crush still applies on top.
WHAT IT IS NOT: no pumps to clear the flood, no repair (the aquarium's restore resets), no hull-form physics (the collapse depth is one number scaled by one scalar), no thermal or acoustic cost of the snorkel beyond the `exposed` flag.

## PASS / FAIL (each is a row of `physics/mechanics/subNeeds-selfcheck.mjs`; every one is SABOTAGED and must go red by name)

1. **Untouched.** A vessel stepped with `needs = null` is bit-identical to `stepSub` over 600 ticks, and the `unlimited` class changes nothing about the physics either (energy constant, no clipping of thrust).
2. **Power.** `P` at 2 m/s trim equals hotel + drag x speed / 0.5 to 1e-6 relative; the hydrodynamic part at 2 x the speed is 8 x (within 1%); `E` is monotone non-increasing while not refilling; never negative, never over capacity.
3. **Empty.** At `E = 0` the applied thrust is 0 whatever the command; the boat coasts as quadratic drag says (2 m/s falls to 0.62 m/s in 60 s: 1/v = 1/v0 + k t); ballast and planes still act.
4. **Refill.** Battery: charges at `chargeW` x dt at 0.5 m, not at 1.5 m. Snorkel: charges at 3.5 m, `exposed` true, not at 5 m. Air-independent: never. Capped at capacity.
5. **Strain and damage.** Strain = depth/collapse. No damage below 0.8; damage grows above it; the collapse depth falls monotonically with damage; a boat with damage 0.6 is crushed at a depth (75 m) an undamaged one (same depth) survives.
6. **Leak.** None at damage 0; rises with depth and with damage; the flooding is capped at 15%; a leaking boat sinks relative to a sound one (depth gain after 60 s at the same commands).
7. **injure** adds, clamps to 1, ignores NaN and negatives.
8. **needsPilot.** Below `low` it heads for the refill depth and the energy rises; above `high` it returns to its task; the hysteresis means it does not flip between them on a single tick; with energy `Infinity` it is the inner pilot unchanged.
9. **The aquarium.** A sub with no needs swims bit-identically to a tank where nothing was called; `setNeeds` and `injure` do what they say; a bad class or amount throws and changes nothing; the snapshot's `needs` is null without it; a sub that runs dry in a long swim stops thrusting and drifts (its speed falls).
10. **The page** (source rows and a live row): the picker, the injure button, the readout.

## A. The four additions (all in `physics/mechanics/subNeeds.mjs`, all OPT-IN: a caller that passes nothing new gets the numbers of v4904, bit for bit)

`stepNeeds(..., extraWeight, aux)` gains an `aux = { pump, repair }` (each 0..1; missing = 0). The needs state gains `exposedS` (seconds spent exposed). Constants are tank-scale, said plainly, and each is a frozen exported record.

1. **FLOOD PUMP.** `aux.pump` removes flooding at `PUMP.rate` (0.01 of the weight per second at full) scaled by `1 - depth / PUMP.headDepth` (90 m: the pump works against the head, so it is useless at the bottom, which is the point), at `PUMP.watts` (1500 W) x the command, and only while energy is over 0. It cannot remove more flood than there is.
2. **REPAIR.** `aux.repair` removes damage at `REPAIR.rate` (0.004 per second at full) for `REPAIR.watts` (800 W) x the command, ONLY at the surface (centre within `REPAIR.maxDepth`, 1.0 m, and speed under `REPAIR.maxSpeed`, 0.5 m/s) and only while energy is over 0. Flood is not repaired by this (that is the pump).
3. **SNORKEL EXPOSURE.** `needs.exposedS` accumulates `dt` while `exposed` (a snorkel hull inside its mast depth). It is a READING and a SCORE TERM for a task; nothing in the physics changes with it. A task decides what exposure costs (piece 3 charges it).
4. **BALLAST PUMP DRAW.** On a model with a ballast tank that has dynamics, the electrical power adds `BALLAST_PUMP.wPerUnit` (600 W) x |change of tank level| / dt. And a boat with NO charge (a limited class at 0) cannot work the normal pump: the tank is frozen at its level (the emergency blow is stored air and still works). On a model without a tank with dynamics nothing changes (the instant ballast of the header is a command, not a pump).

PASS/FAIL, each a row of `subNeeds-selfcheck.mjs` and each SABOTAGED:
* A1 aux absent or all zero is bit-identical to the same call before this round (600 ticks, state and needs).
* A2 pump: flood falls by exactly rate x (1 - depth/head) x dt x command; no pump at 90 m; never below 0; costs `PUMP.watts` x command; does nothing at energy 0; a flooded boat with the pump on ends lighter (depth after 60 s) than the same boat without.
* A3 repair: damage falls at rate x dt at the surface, not at 2 m, not at 1 m/s; costs its watts; never below 0; does nothing at energy 0; the collapse depth rises with it.
* A4 exposure: grows by dt while exposed, only then; a battery boat's never grows; `readings` reports it.
* A5 ballast draw: the watts rise with the level change on a tank model and not on the header model; a dead boat's tank does not move and its blow does.
* A6 `unlimited` and `needs` null unchanged.

## B. Piece 3: the SURVEY RUN (brain/needsTask.mjs), registered here before any pilot is scored on it

**The job.** A tank-scale battery boat with a pre-damaged hull must visit three survey stations within 600 s, IN ANY ORDER (the order is the pilot's choice: it is part of weighing the needs). A station is visited when the centre is within 8 m of it horizontally and 4 m vertically. The boat starts underwater at the origin with a charge and damage the scenario states. The run ends at 600 s, or when crushed.
**Why this puts the two needs in conflict.** Stations are far (so the charge matters), some are deep enough that strain passes the 0.8 yield line of THIS hull (so depth costs hull life, and a damaged hull's collapse depth is lower, down to a station that cannot be reached at all), and the only refill is the surface, which is a trip away from the stations. A pilot that ignores the charge strands; a pilot that ignores the hull creeps its damage up (and a leak adds weight) or is crushed; a pilot that weighs them must choose a station order and skip a station it cannot afford.
**Scenario kinds** (seeded, deterministic, like `subTask`): `plain` (sound hull, shallow stations, full charge: the control that nothing is needed), `deep` (damage 0.2..0.5, stations down to 0.85 of the UNDAMAGED collapse depth: the hull kind), `far` (stations 150..260 m out, start charge 0.35..0.65: the energy kind), `both` (deep stations AND far AND low charge).
**The score** of a run, fixed now: `score = 0.45 x visited/reachable + 0.45 x returned + 0.1 x hull` (hull = 1 - (damage_end - damage_start)/0.5, clamped 0..1), in [0, 1], where `returned` = alive and at the surface (centre under 3 m) at the end; **a crushed boat scores 0** (the boat and its data are lost). A boat alive but flat (charge 0) at depth is not returned. Exposure is reported, and charged only to snorkel hulls (not used here: the task boat is a battery hull). *Revised in the open after the first baseline run:* the first draft weighted 0.7 / 0.2 / 0.1, and a boat that pressed on and STRANDED (alive, no charge, at depth) still scored 0.605 against the refill trip's 0.578 -- a reckless pilot could not be told from a careful one because stranding cost only a fifth of the score. A boat that does not come home is a lost boat, so `returned` now weighs as much as the work (0.45 / 0.45 / 0.1); the baselines were re-scored under it and the old and new numbers are in the gate headers.
**Baseline pilots (rule pilots, the things a stateful pilot must beat):**
* `SPIN` (control): goes to the surface and stays there, working the pump while there is flood and the repair while there is damage -- the SAFE HARBOUR, which never does the job. (First draft held the surface and did nothing; a damaged hull leaks from the first second and the pump-less control sank and was crushed in 2 of 40 runs, so the control was changed to one that cannot die of the leak.) Expected score about 0.55 on every kind (returned 0.45 + the unchanged hull 0.1).
* `DIRECT`: the PD autopilot to the stations in the order they are listed, at the task speed, depth as stated, ignoring the charge and the hull (no pump, no repair).
* `TRIP`: `DIRECT` inside `needsPilot.wrap` with the job's thresholds (surface under 30% of the charge, back to the task over 60% -- the first draft used needsPilot's 90%, which spends the 600 s refilling: 6 kW fills 3 MJ in 500 s -- so the baseline is a trip that fits a timed job). Hull-blind.
* `HULLONLY`: the hull rule alone, no trip -- the control that shows the hull is a need without the charge. The hull rule: a depth cap of 0.75 of the CURRENT collapse depth plus the 3.5 m capture allowance (it reads `readings().collapseDepth`), a station that rule forbids is skipped, the nearest feasible station first, the flood pump while there is flood, repair whenever it is at the surface with charge, and up for the last 60 s.
* `GUARDED`: `HULLONLY` inside the same trip. The best rule pilot written for the job, and the one a stateful pilot has to beat to say it weighs the needs better than a rule does.
**Task validity, checked on the BASELINE set (8 seeds x 4 kinds, seeds 501-508; six was too few -- the standard errors on `both` were as big as the differences) BEFORE any other pilot exists; each is a row of `brain/needsTask-selfcheck.mjs` (kinds plain and deep, and the mechanics) or `brain/needsTaskValidity-selfcheck.mjs` (kinds far and both).** Revised in the open after the first run of the baselines: the first draft's V5 asked `SPIN < DIRECT < TRIP < GUARDED` on `both`, and DIRECT and TRIP both score 0 there because a hull-blind boat is crushed (a damaged hull leaks from the start and a pilot that never pumps sinks), so the two cannot be told apart on that kind; HULLONLY was added so each need is separable on its own kind. The thresholds were written before the run; the structure was changed, not the thresholds.
* V1 a kind and a seed always make the same scenario, a run is deterministic (same bytes), the first station is always within reach, an unknown kind throws;
* V2 `plain`: DIRECT, TRIP, HULLONLY and GUARDED all visit 3 of 3 in every run and score over 0.95 (the job is doable when nothing is in conflict); SPIN scores 0.55 +- 0.05 and visits none;
* V3 `deep`: DIRECT ends crushed in at least 3 of every 10 runs; HULLONLY beats DIRECT by more than max(0.03, 2 se); GUARDED is crushed in none (the hull is a need);
* V4 `far`: DIRECT is stranded or crushed in at least 3 of 10; TRIP beats DIRECT by more than max(0.03, 2 se) (the charge is a need and the trip is a partial answer);
* V5 `both`: GUARDED beats HULLONLY, TRIP and DIRECT each by more than max(0.03, 2 se) and is crushed in none (weighing the two needs beats either alone);
* V6 headroom: no baseline's mean over `both` reaches 0.95 and GUARDED's does not reach 0.9 (there is something left for a better pilot to win);
* V7 a station deeper than the damaged hull's collapse depth can take is marked `unreachable`, and the score's `visited / reachable` ignores it (a pilot is not charged for the impossible); the score of a crushed boat is 0.
If any V fails the TASK is wrong and is changed (in the open, with the old number beside the new), never the threshold; the numbers read off are recorded in the gates' headers with their date.
**What the audit set is.** `TRAIN_SET`, `HELD_OUT_SET`, `AUDIT_SET`, `BASELINE_SET` with disjoint seeds, as in `subTask`. THE AUDIT SET IS NOT READ IN THIS ROUND (nothing here trains); the baselines are scored on `BASELINE_SET`, and the audit set is left unread for piece 4.
**What the verdict rule for piece 4 will be (registered now, used later):** a stateful pilot F beats the rule pilot `GUARDED` on `AUDIT_SET` iff `mean(F) - mean(GUARDED) > max(0.02, 2 sd)` and F is crushed in no more runs than `GUARDED`; the controls are the same circuit with no state, a size-matched random wiring and the same wiring with the energy and hull readings withheld (so that a gain cannot come from anything but weighing the needs).
**NOT CLAIMED:** that any of this is a real boat; that a circuit weighs the needs; that the rule `GUARDED` is the best possible (it is the best written).

# Round 3 (UNNUMBERED): PIECE 4 -- a stateful pilot for the survey run -- REGISTERED 2026-10-27 BEFORE ANY CODE FOR IT AND BEFORE ANY PILOT IS TRAINED OR SCORED (Keith, 2026-10-27: "write the piece 4 registration"; nothing below has been built or run; the training waits for Keith's go after the code has been gated and sabotaged)

Piece 3 (`brain/needsTask.mjs`, `brain/needsTask-selfcheck.mjs`, `brain/needsTaskValidity-selfcheck.mjs`) built the job and five rule pilots and ran no learned pilot on it. This is the registration for the pilot that runs on it. Nothing in the tree yet claims that any circuit weighs the charge and the hull better than a rule does.

## 1. The question, and what a result would and would not mean
**THE QUESTION.** On the survey run, does a pilot whose only memory is a circuit's wiring decide better between the charge and the hull than (a) the same pilot with that wiring's memory removed, (b) pilots whose wiring is a random, type-preserving or degree-preserving rewiring of the same parts, (c) the same pilot with the charge and hull readings withheld, and (d) the hand-written rule `GUARDED`? (a)-(c) ask whether the WIRING is a better reservoir for this job; (d) asks whether any of it is better than a rule a person wrote.
**WHAT A PASS WOULD MEAN:** under this recipe, this wiring held and used the charge and hull better than its controls and than the rule. **WHAT IT WOULD NOT MEAN:** that a fish or a fly "wants" anything (the need comes from the task, not the circuit); that the numbers are a real boat's (tank-scale); that the rule `GUARDED` is the best possible; that the result generalises beyond the seeds and kinds of the survey run.

## 2. Two things measured and one stated BEFORE this registration, because they decide whether the question can be asked at all
* **The task needs memory of some kind (MEASURED, throwaway script, BASELINE_SET kinds far and both, seeds 501-508, the set the task was calibrated on).** `GUARDED` with its refill trip given a SINGLE threshold of 0.45 (surface under 0.45, return over it: no hysteresis) scores far 0.381 and both 0.407; `GUARDED` with the trip's hysteresis (surface under 0.3, return over 0.6) scores 0.850 and 0.813. The paired difference over the 16 runs is 0.437 with a standard error of 0.058. The single-threshold pilot chatters at the threshold. So the job punishes a decision rule that cannot hold its decision.
* **BUT THE HYSTERESIS CAN LIVE IN THE WORLD, NOT IN THE PILOT (STATED, NOT YET MEASURED).** A stateless rule that reads the boat's depth as well as its charge can hold the same decision: at the surface, keep charging until 0.6; down, go up under 0.3. The depth is a memory the world keeps for the pilot. So a NO STATE pilot that is shown the depth is NOT handicapped on the hysteresis, and a stateful pilot's advantage over it, if any, has to come from something else. **This is checked, not assumed (reference `GUARDED-OBS` below): if a stateless rule from the observations reproduces `GUARDED`'s score, the result says the task does not need internal state, and the comparison with NO STATE is read as such.**
* **This is not the premise piece 3's note wrote** ("energy and hull strain are slow internal states which a stateless pilot cannot hold"): the charge and the strain are READINGS the pilot is handed every tick, not states it must integrate. What a circuit could still add is memory of what it DECIDED and of the history of the charge; whether the job needs either is exactly what the `GUARDED-OBS` check and NO STATE answer.

## 3. The pilot (`brain/pilotNeedsCircuit.mjs`, to be built and gated)
**A reservoir pilot at the DECISION level; the PD autopilot flies.** The circuit decides which station to go to, whether to surface, how deep it may go, and whether to run the pump and the repair; `brain/autopilotSub.mjs` wrapped by `brain/surfacePilot.mjs` flies exactly as it does for the rule pilots (the same `base(ctx)` of `brain/needsTask.mjs`). The circuit steps ONCE A SECOND (every 30th control tick; the decision is held between steps), so a 600 s run is 600 reservoir steps and a memory of a hundred steps is a hundred seconds.
**Inputs (21 features, each scaled by a constant registered here, clipped to [-1, 1]):** six global -- the charge fraction; the strain (depth over the CURRENT collapse depth, clipped at 1.2 then divided by 1.2); the damage; the flood times 10; the depth over 60; the time over 600 -- and for each of the three stations in the order the scenario lists them, five -- the distance over 200 (clipped at 1), the cosine and the sine of the bearing relative to the boat's heading, the station's depth over 60, and whether it is visited. A station's `unreachable` flag is NOT an input (the pilot reads the hull and the depths and must work it out, as `GUARDED` does).
**Outputs (7, each clamped to [-1, 1], from the readout of the circuit's output cells):** o0 the work-or-surface residual; o1-o3 a residual to each station's score; o4 the depth-cap residual; o5 the pump residual; o6 the repair residual.
**THE DECODE, AND THE START POINT.** Each decision is a RULE value plus the circuit's residual, so that with every output zero the pilot is the rule `GUARDED-1T` exactly (below): surface iff `0.4 x (0.45 - charge fraction) x 10 + o0 > 0` (so the rule surfaces under 0.45 of the charge) OR the rule's fixed last call (`needsTask`'s RISE_RATE and RISE_MARGIN, NOT learnable) OR no feasible station is left; the station chosen is the argmax of `-distance/200 + o_k`, feasible stations only (feasible = the rule's HULL_DEPTH test), visited and infeasible ones at -9; the depth cap is `(0.9 + 0.05 x o4)` of the current collapse depth, clipped to [0.6, 0.95]; the pump is on iff `(rule: flood > 0.001 and charge > 0.1 ? 1 : -1) + o5 > 0`; the repair is on iff `(rule: at the surface, damaged and charge > 0.1 ? 1 : -1) + o6 > 0`. **The pilot starts as `GUARDED-1T`, bit for bit (a gate row), and training moves it from there**, as the rung 4a pilot started as the autopilot.
**The circuit is the rung 4a reservoir** (`brain/pilotSubCircuitState.mjs`'s update, rewritten for 21 features and 7 outputs, the recurrent weights FIXED): h' = relu(b + W_in x + sigma M h), M the signed synapse-count matrix scaled to largest singular value 1, sigma one trained scalar (start 0.95, clamped to [0, 1]); trained: the input weights (21 x 88), the cells' bias (148), sigma (1), the readout (7 x 64) and its bias (7) = **2,452 numbers**. The circuit is the hemibrain heading loop (`vendor/hemibrain/hemibrain-cx-heading-loop.json`, 148 cells, 7,080 edges, 88 input cells, 64 output cells) and its controls are `brain/pilotSubCircuit.mjs`'s, applied unchanged (the type is the `layer`).

## 4. The circuits (12) and what each says
* `cx` -- the real wiring, with memory.
* `nostate` -- the SAME pilot with the state zeroed before every update (no recurrence at all); the control that says what the memory buys, whatever the wiring. It is shown the depth.
* `withheld` -- `cx` with the charge, strain, damage and flood inputs set to zero (the pilot still sees depth, time and the stations): the control that says whether any gain comes from the NEEDS. It starts as `GUARDED-1T` too and can learn only what the remaining inputs allow.
* `er:0-2`, `layer:0-2`, `degree:0-2` -- three controls of each verdict family (the minimum `MIN_CONTROLS` the verdict rule needs); SIGN and COUNT are left out (they were reported-only in rung 4a and cost 40 percent more).

## 6. Reference pilots and checks, run BEFORE any training and recorded in the results file
* **`GUARDED-1T`** (the start point): `GUARDED` with the trip at the single threshold 0.45. **`GUARDED`** (the rule to beat). **`GUARDED-OBS`**: the stateless-from-observations rule of section 2 (surface under 0.3 or, when the boat is at the surface (depth under 3 m), while the charge is under 0.6; otherwise as `GUARDED`). `SPIN`, `HULLONLY`, `TRIP`, `DIRECT` for the record.

* **MEMORY-NEED CHECK:** on the three audits' kinds far and both, `mean(GUARDED) - mean(GUARDED-1T) > max(0.03, 2 se)` (the section-2 measurement, repeated on fresh seeds). If not, the job does not punish the lack of a held decision and the experiment is not run.

* **TASK RE-CHECK:** `GUARDED` beats `SPIN` on far and both on each audit by more than max(0.03, 2 se), and `DIRECT` is crushed in at least 3 of 12 deep runs. If this fails, the task did not generalise beyond the seeds it was tuned on and the result is reported as that.

## 7. Training (one recipe for every circuit, copied from rung 4a and scaled to the cost)
The sparse (1+1)-ES of rung 4a: 4 searches x **300** children (rung 4a used 400; each child here is 16 survey dives, about 2.4 s), sigma 0.3, 1 to 4 weights perturbed per child, pruned toward the start, seeds fixed per circuit (`seedBase + 10 x ordinal`), fitness = the mean survey-run score on TRAIN. **Eligibility:** a search's best is eligible iff it is crushed in no more POOL runs than `GUARDED-1T` is. **Champion:** the eligible search with the best mean on HELD_OUT; no eligible search -> the circuit is scored as `GUARDED-1T` (its start). The champion is scored ONCE on each audit. Cost: 12 circuits x 4 searches x 300 children x 2.4 s = about 9.6 core-hours, about 2.5 hours on this box's four cores.

## 8. The verdict (rung 4a's rule, turned for higher-is-better, plus the rule comparison)
* **A beats B on an audit** iff `mean(A) - mean(B) > max(0.02, 2 sd of B's family)` (a family of one has sd 0). `cx` is compared with each verdict family (ER, TYPE-BLOCK, DEGREE: the three circuits' scores on that audit).
* **WIRING VERDICT:** PASS iff `cx` beats ER, TYPE-BLOCK and DEGREE on all three audits; FAIL iff against the strongest family it wins on fewer than two audits; otherwise IN BETWEEN. NOT ASSESSED below three finished controls per family.
* **RULE VERDICT (separate, always printed):** `cx` BEATS THE RULE iff on every audit `mean(cx) - mean(GUARDED) > max(0.02, 2 se of the paired difference)` and `cx` is crushed in no more audit runs than `GUARDED`.
* **READINGS, registered now:** (a) START CHECK, MEMORY-NEED and TASK RE-CHECK as in section 6: a failure of any is the first line of the report and the verdicts below it are not weighed. (b) **NO STATE and WITHHELD:** `cx` beats NO STATE on an audit iff the same margin rule; likewise WITHHELD. If `cx` does NOT beat NO STATE the memory buys nothing here; if it does not beat WITHHELD the needs readings buy nothing here. Both are reported whatever the wiring verdict. ## 1. The question, and what a result would and would not mean
**THE QUESTION.** Take the champions Round 3 trained (the pilots of `cx`, `nostate`, `withheld` and the nine controls). (b) Does the real wiring gain MORE from the advisor than its rewired controls do? (c) Does the advised brain beat the advisor alone (does the brain add anything to the rule)? (d) Is any gain the advisor's CONTENT, or would a delayed copy of it do as well?

## 2. The advisor and the socket
* **THE ADVISOR (primary): `GUARDED`**, the rule of Round 2 (`flyDecision(ruleSeat("hyst"))`: the refill trip at 0.3 / 0.6 with its own held flag, the last call, the nearest feasible station, the pump when flooded with charge above 0.1, the repair at the surface). * **THE COMBINATION RULES (modes), each a pure function of the circuit's decision `c` and the advisor's decision `a`, field by field (a decision is `{ surface, station, cap, pump, repair }`):**
  - `ALONE`: `c`.
  - `VETO` (the conscience; every field at once): `surface = c.surface OR a.surface`; `pump = c.pump OR a.pump`; `repair = c.repair OR a.repair`; `cap = min(c.cap, a.cap)`; `station = c.station` unless it is visited or infeasible (the rule's HULL_DEPTH test), in which case `a.station`. # Round 19 -- is the twin's gain a constant, or does it depend on the seed block? A meta-analysis of every fresh-seed measurement of it (REGISTERED 2026-11-02, before any code; PART OF THE DATA IS ALREADY READ, said below)

## 1. Why, and what is already known
The twin's gain over the tuned rule (full twin minus TUNED-R8, gauge sd 0.10, three stations, 600-second dives, far and both) has been measured on fresh seeds again and again: **+0.0214 (Round 12, 288 dives, se 0.0040), +0.0230 (Round 13's exact arm, 192, se 0.0048), +0.0078 (Round 14, 192, se 0.0036), +0.0211 (Round 15 at sd 0.10, 192, se 0.0046)**. Rounds 16 (three stations), 17 (the full twin, 288 dives) and the other arms of those rounds also contain the same comparison on their own fresh seeds and have not been read. I have therefore ALREADY SEEN the first four. A heterogeneity statistic on those four alone, which I computed while choosing this round, is Q = 10.1 on 3 degrees of freedom (p about 0.02): the four estimates differ by more than their stated standard errors say they should. So the question is no longer open for four blocks; what the round adds is the other blocks, which are unread, and the proper estimate. A pre-registration that is part retrospective is said to be one; the verdict below is read on ALL blocks and the expectations cover the whole.
Why it matters: every verdict in this series has been a pooled paired difference over a margin of 2 standard errors computed from the dives themselves. If the gain differs between seed blocks more than that standard error allows, the standard errors in Rounds 11-18 are too small, and some KEEPS / LOSES readings were closer calls than they looked.

## 2. The blocks
Each block is one set of 96 far-and-both dives (48 seeds of each kind) flown with the full twin and with TUNED-R8 under the SAME conditions (gauge sd 0.10, three stations, the shipped task): **Round 12 as one block** (288 dives, from its report: +0.0214, se 0.0040: the per-dive data were not kept); **Round 13: V1, V2; Round 14: W1, W2; Round 15: U1, U2 (the sd-0.10 arms); Round 16: T1, T2 (the 3-station arms); Round 17: S1, S2, S3**. That is 12 blocks, 11 of them from the arm files of the rounds (per-dive scores, fingerprints), 1 from a report. A block's estimate is the mean of twin minus tuned over its dives and its standard error the standard error of that paired mean.

## 3. The statistics (inverse-variance weights w = 1 / se^2 throughout)
The fixed-effect mean xbar = sum(w x) / sum(w); **Cochran's Q** = sum(w (x - xbar)^2) on k - 1 = 11 degrees of freedom, with its chi-square upper-tail **p**; **I-squared** = max(0, (Q - df) / Q); the DerSimonian-Laird **tau-squared** = max(0, (Q - df) / (sum(w) - sum(w^2) / sum(w))); the **random-effects mean** with weights 1 / (se^2 + tau^2) and its standard error; the **inflation factor** sqrt(max(1, Q / df)) by which a block's stated standard error should be multiplied to account for the between-block variation.

## 4. The verdicts
* **Heterogeneity:** `THE TWIN'S GAIN VARIES BETWEEN SEED BLOCKS BEYOND CHANCE` iff p < 0.05; `THE TWIN'S GAIN IS CONSISTENT ACROSS SEED BLOCKS` otherwise.
* **Estimate:** `THE BEST ESTIMATE OF THE GAIN IS +<random-effects mean> (se <its se>; 12 BLOCKS)`, and `IT IS REAL` iff the random-effects mean exceeds 2 of its standard errors and is positive; `IT IS NOT DISTINGUISHABLE FROM ZERO` otherwise.
* **Calibration:** `THE STATED STANDARD ERRORS ARE TOO SMALL BY A FACTOR OF <inflation>` iff the factor exceeds 1.2; `THE STATED STANDARD ERRORS ARE ABOUT RIGHT` otherwise.
* **REPORTED, NOT IN THE VERDICT:** each block's estimate, se and weight; the fixed-effect mean; Q, df, p, I-squared, tau; the leave-one-block-out range of the random-effects mean; the same statistics for the two kinds (far, both) separately.

## 5. Expectations, recorded BEFORE any unread block (Rounds 16 and 17) is read
Heterogeneity beyond chance across the 12 blocks: about 55 percent (four known blocks at p 0.02; eight unread blocks of the same design, the same gauge and the same twin should be closer to chance). The random-effects mean lies between +0.012 and +0.020: about 75 percent; it is REAL: about 90. The inflation factor exceeds 1.2: about 45 percent. I-squared above 30 percent: about 50. The leave-one-out range of the random-effects mean is narrower than 0.004: about 70.

## 6. What will be built
`tools/ship/plannerMeta.mjs` (library: the statistics, the chi-square tail, the block estimates and the report, no file reads), `plannerMetaCmd.mjs` (export-free: `run`, reads the arm files of Rounds 13-17 and Round 12's reported block) and its gate with a mutation pass (the chi-square tail against known values, Q and tau on hand fixtures); then the run once Rounds 16 and 17 have finished, and the result as section 7. **NOT IN THIS ROUND:** any new dive.
