# Submarine countermeasures: a decoy, a bubble screen and an anti-torpedo interceptor, and a registered read of what they do in the arenas (2026-11-06; UNNUMBERED; REGISTERED BEFORE ANY CODE FOR IT IS WRITTEN AND BEFORE ANY GEOMETRY OF ITS SETS IS FLOWN)

Keith, 2026-11-06, asked "can subs have anti-torpedoes, like chaff?" and then "lets queue up and proceed to do all those items" -- items T1 to T4 of the queue. `physics/mechanics/subTorpedo.mjs` already says what is missing ("no decoys or countermeasures ... each an addition to this file, not a change to it"). This note registers the additions, the numbers I chose for them, and one read.

**THE NUMBERS BELOW ARE MY CHOICES, NOT MEASUREMENTS.** Nothing in the tree says how loud a real decoy is, how big a bubble curtain is or how fast an anti-torpedo runs; I picked values that make each mechanism plausible against the torpedo's own numbers (6.5 m/s cruise, 90 s burn, a 35-degree seeker cone that hears the LOUDEST thing in it, a lock that drops after 3 s of silence, a proximity fuse of 8 m). A result of this note is a statement about THESE parameters in THESE arenas. It is not a design recommendation for a real submarine, and a different set of numbers could read the other way.

## 1. What exists (read from the code, not recalled)

* The torpedo (`stepTorpedo`): runs straight, looks at 5 Hz, takes the target with the highest SNR inside its cone, homes by proportional navigation, detonates at the closest approach inside 8 m (armed after 40 m). It hears a boat from `K.sourceLevel(speed)` (100 dB at 0.5 m/s, 112 at 2, 119 at 4.5) and a launched torpedo is a source of 125 dB (`K.SONAR.torpedoSL`).
* What a boat hears of a torpedo: a contact `{ kind: "torpedo", bearing, elevation, snr }`, no range. The SNR is the only distance information: for a boat at 2 m/s a torpedo is 32 dB at 100 m, 26 at 200 m, 20 at 400 m, 16 at 600 m (plus 3 dB of luck). The torpedo covers 6.5 m/s, so 400 m is about 60 s away.
* The two arenas: `brain/subDuel.mjs` (two boats, four torpedoes each, one decided by the first sinking; **in a calibration of 24 baseline duels of the default tactician against itself, 96 torpedoes were fired, 24 hit, 24 boats were sunk, and seat A won 20**: a duel is a torpedo race, which is why a countermeasure has room to matter there) and `brain/seaOgre.mjs` (three defenders against the OGRE, whose tubes fire a torpedo about every 7 s while submerged; **the baseline fights on seeds 1 to 6 had the OGRE fire 2 or 3 torpedoes each**: there is little for a countermeasure to defeat; the escorts burst on a boat and are not torpedoes).
* The tactician (`brain/subTactics.mjs`) answers `{ depth, heading, speed, fire, ping }`; a heard torpedo makes it turn off the bearing, go to 4 m/s and change depth.

## 2. What is added (every one default OFF; with all of them off the flights are byte-identical, held by fingerprint rows)

* **Decoy (T1).** A boat releases a noisemaker at its own position: a source of **130 dB** (a boat at full speed is 119; a launched torpedo 125) that drifts at **1.2 m/s** on a heading 90 degrees off the releasing boat's, alternating sides, at the boat's depth, for **40 s**. A boat carries **3**, at least **6 s** apart. The seeker's rule is unchanged (loudest in the cone) and so it prefers the decoy whenever the decoy is in the cone: the change to `stepTorpedo` is one line, a target may carry `sl`, which replaces `K.sourceLevel(speed)`. A torpedo that detonates on a decoy damages nothing; the arena says so.
* **Bubble screen (T2).** A boat releases a curtain at its own position: a sphere of **20 m** radius that stays where it was released for **30 s**; a boat carries **2**. For the seeker a target whose line from the torpedo passes through the sphere has its SNR reduced by **20 dB** (one screen counts once). It does not affect the fuse (physical). The change to `stepTorpedo` is an option `screens`.
* **Anti-torpedo interceptor (T3).** A boat launches a small, fast, short-lived torpedo along the bearing of a heard torpedo: cruise **9 m/s**, burn **20 s** (180 m), turns **0.4 rad/s**, a **50-degree** half-cone, armed after **15 m**, a fuse of **8 m**, the torpedo's own ears; it listens for LAUNCHED TORPEDOES (125 dB). A boat carries **2**. A torpedo it detonates on is destroyed. The change to `stepTorpedo` is an option `params` that overrides the torpedo constants for one body.
* **The tactician (T4).** A WRAPPER around any tactician (`withCountermeasures(tactician, cm)`), not an edit of `subTactics.mjs` (its theta vector is searched and pinned): when a heard torpedo's SNR first reaches **decoySnr 22 dB** (about 330 m) it adds `decoy: true` to the order; at **screenSnr 22** `screen: true`; at **interceptSnr 24** (about 270 m) `intercept: { bearing }`; the order is otherwise the wrapped tactician's. The arena reads the three flags when its options enable the mechanism. The three SNR thresholds are the `cm` parameters of a side.
* The hooks in `brain/subDuel.mjs` and `brain/seaOgre.mjs` are additive (an option `countermeasures` that is absent by default); the new module `brain/subCountermeasures.mjs` holds the three entities, their constants (`CM`), the wrapper and the report lines. Built and gated together because they share the plumbing, each with its own section of the gate and its own mutants.

## 3. THE REGISTERED READ

> In the two arenas, on fresh geometries, does a side that carries each countermeasure (and all three) do better than the same side without them?

**Arena A (the duel).** Side X = the default tactician (`THETA_DEFAULT`) with the PD autopilot and the countermeasure under test; side Y = the same without. **24 geometries, geometry seeds 801 to 824** (`subDuel.scenario(seed, "patrol")`, which `subTournament.geometries(24, 801)` makes; no set of this tree used seeds above 6960 for the duel), each flown twice, X in each seat (48 duels per matchup). Four matchups: decoy only, screen only, interceptor only, all three. Reading (the tournament's own): the paired edge of X over Y (the mean over geometries of the mean of the two seats' score differences), `z` = edge over its paired standard error; **X BEATS Y iff the edge is positive and z >= 2, IS BELOW iff negative and z <= -2, else TIES.** Printed with them and not part of any verdict: wins, draws and losses, torpedoes decoyed, screened (a look that lost its target to a screen) and intercepted, the boats lost by X and by Y, and the seat bias.

**Arena B (the Sea OGRE).** Three defenders with `GUNNER_THETA`, with and without all three countermeasures, **scenarios 101 to 112** (twelve, nothing has used them). Reading: the paired difference of the fight's own score (with minus without), BETTER iff above 2 se (the board's rule); printed: defended / breached / holdout, defenders lost and OGRE torpedoes decoyed or intercepted, in each arm.

**Sensitivity, printed and NOT weighed:** arena A with the three release thresholds (22, 22, 24 dB) each moved to 18 and to 28 together.

**Checks (a failure and the readings are NOT WEIGHED):** every arm flew the same geometries; no score is non-finite; **a side without countermeasures flown with the countermeasure code present is byte-identical to the old code** (the fingerprint of every duel and fight); the side X against ITSELF with no countermeasure has an edge of exactly 0 (the pairing is sound); the mechanisms FIRE in the flights read (at least one decoy released by X in the decoy matchup, one screen in the screen matchup, one interceptor in the interceptor matchup) -- otherwise the matchup is a read of nothing and is said to be.

**Expectations, written before any geometry is flown (and before DEV beyond the gate's fixtures).**
* Arena A: decoy BEATS: about 80 percent; screen BEATS: about 30 (a 20 m curtain left behind a boat that moves away covers it for a few seconds); interceptor BEATS: about 60; all three BEATS: about 85; any matchup IS BELOW: about 7.
* Arena A: with all three, X is sunk in fewer duels than Y by at least 8 of the 48: about 70.
* Arena B: the paired score TIES (little to defeat): about 65 percent; BETTER: 25; WORSE: 10. The defended count differs by at most 1 of 12: about 60.
* The mechanisms fire in the flights read (the check): about 95.
* The sensitivity at 18 dB (an early release) is no worse than 22 for the decoy: about 55.

**What a result would not say.** That a real submarine should carry any of these, or that a decoy of 130 dB works: the parameters are mine. That the tactician's rule (a threshold on a heard SNR) is a good one: it is the simplest one and was not tuned. That a decoy beats a smarter torpedo: this seeker takes the loudest thing in its cone and has no classifier, no range gate and no memory of what it locked first. Anything about the Sea OGRE beyond twelve scenarios in which the OGRE fires two or three torpedoes.

## AMENDMENT B (2026-11-06; made on DEV geometries 1 to 3, BEFORE either registered set was flown; DEV is not a result)

The DEV run of the command read the SCREEN as `BEATS` the same side without it on an edge of +0.0006 (z 2.49), the sunk counts equal at 3 and 3 and the wins 3 and 3. That is the registered rule applied literally (positive edge, z >= 2) to a difference of six ten-thousandths of a point that is nothing but a few ticks of the speed bonus, and the paired standard error of a list of nearly equal numbers is nearly zero, so z means nothing there. **The reading now needs both: the edge at least the tournament's own DECISIVE (0.05, one net hit: less than that is a draw in every table of this tree) and z at least 2 (BEATS), or the edge at most -0.05 and z at most -2 (IS BELOW); else TIES.** The forecasts of section 3 and Amendment A stand (they were about whether a countermeasure changes who is sunk, which this threshold does not touch). The arena-B rule (the board's: BETTER iff the paired mean is above 2 se) has the same weakness in principle and is NOT changed, because its scores are on the fight's own scale and the DEV fights tied exactly (se 0, mean 0), which the board reads as TIES.
