What this is. Seven lumps. blobFieldAt sums them into a field — that field is the blob, and it is a closed form, exact at every instant. box3d moves the seven centres. The formula travels with them, so nothing is ever baked, advected, or smeared.

Why not a grid. Bake the field into 64³ and advect it out-and-back so it ends where it started: the peak falls to 75.3% → 63.0% → 55.8% → 50.5% while the mass stays constant. He does not evaporate — he dissolves. That is numerical diffusion, and it is the heat equation: the measured D = 0.01141, dead constant at every timestep. The aquarium was never at zero kelvin.

It is not a Kelvin, and it cannot be. Einstein's relation is D = kT/γ — the fluctuation-dissipation theorem. A temperature is a ratio of random kicks to drag. Shove a box3d body and let go: its speed reads 1, 1, 1, 1. There is no drag. With γ = 0 there is no dissipation, so there is no temperature to name — only kicks with nothing to be in equilibrium with. Worse: a position random walk is the overdamped limit, which assumes γ → ∞. This slider and this solver assume opposite physics. The slider says D, and D is what it means.

The warmth slider is real. Einstein 1905: <x²> = 2Dt per axis. It jitters the seven centres, not a grid. The peak wanders — sometimes above where it started, because lumps drift together and overlap builds a taller peak. That is what a temperature looks like: fluctuation, not decay. And it has a floor the grid does not have: the peak can never fall below the tallest single lump's amplitude, because each lump keeps its amplitude forever.

The one number that matters. box3d gets a collision radius, not the field radius. Hand it the field radius and it blows the blob apart 5.4× in one second — correctly, because a rigid solver's job is to remove interpenetration and a metaball's peak is interpenetration. Two correct things wanting opposite outcomes. coreScale = 0.15 is where they agree, and it is a choice, not a discovery.
