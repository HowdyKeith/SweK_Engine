Sky — precomputed atmospheric scattering

Rayleigh and Mie single scattering, integrated live against a precomputed transmittance table (render/atmosphere.mjs; method from Bruneton and Neyret as three-geospatial packages it, MIT). The table is built on the CPU by the same code the gate grades against four closed forms — the analytic vertical optical depth, the multiplicative property, the phase normalisations, and the flat-planet secant limit. Single scattering only: twilight and the band just above the horizon come from the second and later orders, and they are not here.

A fisheye of the whole sky dome, from 0.5 km up. The sun's colour and the horizon's are computed, not chosen.

What is not here

Multiple scattering, which is what makes twilight and the bright band above the horizon. Without it the sky is too dark near the horizon and goes black too quickly at dusk — an absence with a known shape rather than a mystery. The transmittance table would serve aerial perspective (scattering over a finite distance, for objects inside the atmosphere) equally well; no caller asks for it yet.
