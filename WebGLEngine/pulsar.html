Pulsar — a neutron star as a clock

A neutron star spins and sweeps two beams from its magnetic poles. Each time a beam crosses the line of sight to us, a pulse arrives. Real pulsars are among the most precise clocks in the universe, and they spin down — the period lengthens as the star loses energy. This runs physics/pulsar/pulsar.js: the pulse arrival times solve the timing model with only arithmetic and a square root, so the pulse train is bit-identical on every architecture — folded into the fingerprint, it ticks the same on every box in the fleet.
