Meijer G-function — and the family that grades it

Evaluated by residue sum, not by integrating the Mellin-Barnes contour: closing the contour and summing residues at the poles of the first m gamma factors gives an exact linear combination of generalized hypergeometrics. Different algorithm, different limits, stated rather than implied.
The reason G is worth having here: nearly every classical special function is a G at particular parameters, so the answer key is a family. A key that only tested e^-z would pass an implementation that ignored half its parameters — e^-z is m=1,n=0,p=0,q=1 and exercises none of them. Each row below is computed independently and compared pointwise.

2. Cancellation — why a plausible number can be worthless

Double precision carries ~16 decimal digits. An alternating series whose largest term is 10c times its own sum has spent c of them. e^-z through its own series is exact at z=2, wrong in the 10th digit at z=8, and at z=20 the answer is off by more than itself — while still being a finite, plausible-looking float. The evaluator reports this before you compare anything.

4. The instrument — a distribution measured, not a function evaluated

If X1..Xn are independent Gamma(ai,1), the product has an exact law and that law is a Meijer G — pdf G^{n,0}_{0,n}, cdf G^{n,1}_{1,n+1}, a different family. So we can sample the product by Monte Carlo and grade the samples against a law nobody tabulated. Four independent routes must agree: the sampler, the pdf, the cdf, and the moments (∏Γ(a+k)/Γ(a), no G in it at all).

A KS test that passes proves little on its own — D always shrinks with n and always looks small. The instrument only earns the name if the same samples are rejected against a law that is subtly wrong. That is what the sabotage button is for.

5. Ratios — where the reference is ELEMENTARY

Z = X/Y with X~Gamma(a,1), Y~Gamma(b,1). Via G this is 1/(z Γ(a)Γ(b)) · G^{1,1}_{1,1}(1/z | 1-a ; b); the witness is the beta-prime density z^(a-1) / (B(a,b)(1+z)^(a+b)), which contains nothing harder than a Beta. The product instrument could only check G against G plus moments — here a residue-sum bug has nowhere to hide. It also exercises a family the product never touched: n=1, so an upper parameter is used, and the argument is 1/z rather than z.

The hole at z = 1 is real and inherent, not a tuning problem. p = q = 1 gives radius of convergence 1, so the G needs |1/z| < 1. The reciprocal identity f_Z(z) = f_Z'(1/z)/z² with shapes swapped turns z < 1 into z' > 1 — but it moves the argument across 1, it does not make it smaller. Both branches refuse near z = 1. The elementary form covers the gap and is what a caller should use; the G is the thing being checked wherever it converges.
