Limiting in two dimensions

In 1D the limiter bought monotonicity exactly — zero new extrema, an exact boolean. In 2D a slope becomes a gradient, and the reconstruction's corners add both slopes at once, which 1D has no equivalent of. So per-axis limiting should escape the neighbourhood.
It does not, and that is the round. The corner offsets are ±½ in each axis, so a corner takes half of each slope. With |sx| ≤ dx and |sy| ≤ dy the worst corner excess is (dx+dy)/2 against a half-span of max(dx,dy) — and the mean of two numbers never exceeds their maximum. Two lines, and the prediction this round was built on is refuted.

1. Four gradients on one field

The bound is attained, not comfortable. Evaluated at the cell's own corner, minmod does produce escapes — and they are one ulp. (dx+dy)/2 = max(dx,dy) exactly whenever the two differences are equal, so the guarantee holds with zero margin. The clean zero above comes from subcell centres sitting strictly inside the cell — at ratio 4 the outermost is at 0.375, not 0.5.

2. Where the guarantee actually ends

The guarantee is about the cell, not about the slope. At an offset of 1 — the neighbouring cell centre, which is where an unsplit corner-transport step samples — per-axis minmod escapes its own neighbourhood by a third of the local span. Barth–Jespersen buys nothing out there either, because its constraint is stated on the same corners.
And the field that breaks it is the smooth one. On a diagonal step minmod zeroes the slope at the discontinuity and never leaves, at any offset. A step is the fixture everyone reaches for when testing a limiter, and it is the one that cannot show this.
