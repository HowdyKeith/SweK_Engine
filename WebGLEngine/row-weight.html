The knob nobody asked about

The previous round named the obvious next step: move the row's evaluation point onto the wall. The offset is a closed form, so the correction is one line. It is done here — and it is worth 3.5% in the wrong direction.
And looking for that 3.5% found something worth 1.7×: the row's weight, a hardcoded 1 through six rounds that nobody — including me — ever asked about. A knob with no key, sitting in the middle of every measurement in this thread. The unexamined constant was larger than every effect being measured around it.

1. The projection does exactly what it was supposed to

The constraint becomes exactly satisfiable — thirteen orders, 1.7e-2 down to 5.6e-16. It is now true at the point it is written at.

2. And the answer gets worse

A constraint being exactly true is not the same as a fit being better. A least-squares fit doesn't solve the row — it trades it against the data rows, and moving the point moves the lever arms in that trade. The order is untouched either way.

3. The knob

There is an interior optimum and the default is not it — weight 0.25 reads 6.72e-4 against the default's 1.14e-3, a factor of 1.70, bracketed on both sides. And an over-weighted true constraint destroys almost all of its own benefit: at weight 64 it is barely better than having no row at all. Forcing a true condition crowds out the data that would have told you the rest of the answer.
The projection's 3.5% is itself a function of the weight and vanishes at the good one — so the thing I went looking for only exists at the weight I hadn't examined. The default is left at 1 deliberately, so every earlier number in this thread reproduces. Not claimed: that 0.25 is right — it is the minimum for one field on one mesh at one refinement, and a weight fitted to a fixture is a knob with a story rather than a key. What is claimed is that the default was never chosen, and that it is now visible. Graded by physics/mesh/rowWeight-selfcheck.mjs from gates.html.
