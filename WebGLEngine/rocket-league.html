🚗 Rocket League RL — the brain/rl spike

A continuous-control head trained by a real-physics reward. Point it at your dumped RocketSim collision meshes, prepare, and run a training loop — or run the built-in kinematic stub with no setup. ← back to the panel

What this is (and is not). A feasibility spike: it proves a car-control policy can be driven by a physics reward and improve. The stub backend is a point-mass stand-in that runs anywhere; the rocketsim backend is ground-truth Rocket League physics. It is not a competitive bot — that is millions of steps of training — and it is not MIRA: a policy that “wins” inside a world model has only fooled the model. RocketSim is the ground truth.

1 · Downloads & references

  • RocketSim (the physics) — pip install RocketSim · github.com/ZealanL/RocketSim (you can also use the Install button below)
  • Collision meshes — not redistributable. Dump them once from an installed Rocket League with the RocketSim mesh dumper, then point step 2 at the resulting collision_meshes folder.
  • RLGym / RLGym-sim (higher-level RL env, optional) — rlgym.org
  • Rocket Science dataset (10k hours, for imitation/offline experiments) — huggingface.co/datasets/kyutai/rocket-science
  • MIRA (the world model — reference only) — github.com/mira-wm/mira

Honest limits: the stub is a ground driver, not Rocket League (no car body, aerials or ball spin) — it exercises the loop. The trainer is Evolution Strategies, a starter that needs no gradients and fans out over the fleet; PPO/self-play is the upgrade once real physics is in the loop.
