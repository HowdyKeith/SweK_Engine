One quadratic TrueType font, packed once into a curve texture (Bezier control points) and a band texture, is drawn below at eight sizes from 8 px to a wall-filling title — the same bytes, no atlas rebuilt and no size-dependent baking, because the fragment shader reads the outline and solves for coverage per pixel. That is the point of Slug: text stays sharp at any size and in perspective. The cost is a per-fragment loop over the curves in a band (measured 5–15 tests/sample), so it is the right tool for UI, labels and titles and the wrong one for a static wall of body text. Cross-check vs CPU renders one glyph to a float buffer, reads it back, and compares coverage to slugEval.slugRender at matching points — the on-GPU check the library shipped without.
