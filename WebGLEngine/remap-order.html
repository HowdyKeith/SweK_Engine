Conservation, order, overshoot — pick two

The previous round found you cannot have both: a remap that keeps the sum loses the value, and it was only first order. A second-order conservative remap exists, so this builds it — reconstruct a straight line inside each coarse cell whose cell average is the stored value, then take the fine subcells' averages of that line. Conservation is then exact for any slope whatsoever, because the subcell offsets are symmetric about the cell centre and the slope term cancels in the mean.
Which also means the round trip is the identity for every slope, and proves nothing. Said in advance this time: the previous round discovered the same shape halfway through and had to relabel a result as a non-result. The slopes are graded against analytic cell averages — a truth no operator here is told.

1. The three slopes

The unlimited slope invents field the data never contained — 18.75% of the range on a step, and it does it on a smooth gaussian peak too, so "only near shocks" would have been false here. In an EM grid a value outside the range of its own data is not a rounding error. It is a source.

2. The orders

Piecewise constant is first order, minmod is second, and the unlimited slope is third — I had predicted second. A centred-slope reconstruction of cell averages is third order in the average because the leading error term is symmetric about the cell centre and cancels. Had I asserted "second order" with a tolerance wide enough to pass, this would have shipped a true check hiding a wrong belief.
So the trade is relocated, not defeated: order 3 with overshoot, order 2 without, or order 1 without. You cannot have three and no overshoot — Godunov's barrier turning up in a remap operator. And the place you pay is the peak of the wave, which is the part of the field anybody wanted to refine around. That is the honest end of this thread rather than a win.
