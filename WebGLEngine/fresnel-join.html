Four Fresnels — and a fifth wearing the name

This engine declares Fresnel reflection in three modules and Fresnel's diffraction integrals in a fourth. Until this page nothing had ever compared any of them. Two return amplitude, three return power, and one pair is a bit-for-bit duplicate.
Between physics/em/maxwell.mjs and physics/render/fresnel.mjs the difference is amplitude versus power — a square, so the two numbers differ by a factor of five at a glass interface (−0.2 and 0.04) and neither is wrong. Between physics/render and water/waterMath.js it is a genuine duplicate. Between all of those and physics/optics/fresnel.js there is no relationship at all — those are the Fresnel integrals of near-field diffraction. Same man, different subject. Joining them because both say "fresnel" is exactly how a bad key gets built.

1. Five routes to one number

the raw column is what a naive comparison sees; the squared column is the join
The two amplitude routes also disagree in sign. At normal incidence there is no plane to tell s from p apart, so the sign is pure convention and the two files chose opposite ones. Comparing them directly would report a fault that does not exist.

2. Schlick against the exact law

▬ exact R (unpolarised) ▬ exact Rp ▬ Schlick
The honest statement is not "Schlick is 4% off." At Brewster's angle the exact p reflectance is exactly zero and Schlick has no polarisation in it, so the zero is not inaccurate — it is absent, and the relative error there is infinite. Past the critical angle the exact reflectance is 1 over a whole range and Schlick reads a few percent, a factor of 24 at the critical angle itself. Schlick approximates the unpolarised average in the external-reflection case; the two features that make Fresnel worth having are missing from it, not approximated by it.
