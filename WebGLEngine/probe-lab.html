Probe Lab

A probe released on a timelike geodesic around a Schwarzschild mass — the matter case completing the light case in the geodesic instrument. Both obey almost the same equation; the probe just carries one extra Newtonian term, and everything relativistic lives in the 3Mu².

The orbit

Units are geometric, M = 1. The white circle is the horizon at 2M, the dashed ring is the ISCO at 6M. Untick nothing and the ellipse turns; tick Newtonian and it must close exactly — any turning there would be the integrator, not the spacetime.

Probe telemetry — what actually comes back

Fly a probe and read its log. Everything else on this page computes a trajectory; this records what an onboard clock and spectrometer would say along it. The clock carries an exact key: an orbiting probe runs at √(1−3M/r) while one held still at the same radius runs at √(1−2M/r) — two versus three, and the difference is its speed.

What the probe reports from its distance

A static observer's clock rate and redshift, as a function of where it is holding station.

Driving it

A controller is told a target radius and nothing else. Each trial it releases the probe and observes one bit — did it drift in, or out — then bisects. The exact answer L = r√(M/(r−3M)) is used only afterwards, to mark the paper. Inside the ISCO the orbit still exists, so the controller still finds the right number and reports it unstable: right number, useless plan. At or inside the photon sphere it refuses rather than returning its closest attempt.

Missions — the measurement adjudicates

A proposer states an objective; the controller attempts it; the physics decides. The proposer can be a model in the roundhouse, a script, or you — the adjudicator does not care which, and contains no model calls at all. Note that not everything succeeds: a planner that only ever reported achieved would be a yes-man with a physics engine attached.
