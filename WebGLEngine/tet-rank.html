The 2D answer does not transfer

The previous round found that on a triangulated rectangle the set of cells whose gradient cannot be determined from cell averages at all is exactly two, at every mesh size, while the boundary itself grows without bound. It called the three-dimensional version a genuine open question rather than a guess.
In 3D it is 6n. A gradient has three unknowns and each face neighbour supplies one equation, so a tetrahedron needs three non-coplanar neighbours where a triangle needed two non-collinear ones. A tet has four faces, so there is margin — and more ways to fall short. Measured: 24, 36, 48, 72, 96 at n = 4, 6, 8, 12, 16. def/n = 6.00 in every row.

1. The scaling

The deficient set tracks the box's edges; the boundary tracks its faces. Deficiency needs two box faces at once — a tet on one face loses a neighbour and still has three, which is enough — and the places where two faces meet are the twelve edges, whose total length is O(n). Measured, not assumed: every deficient tet sits on an edge, none in the interior, and 6n over twelve edges is n/2 per edge.
So refinement makes the defect rarer and more numerous at the same time. The fraction falls from 6.25% to 0.39% while the count rises from 24 to 96. A solver that handles them one at a time has more work every time it refines; a solver that ignores them has a smaller fraction wrong. Both are true and they are about different things.

2. Three neighbours are enough

Every rank-3 tet is exact — including the 672 boundary tets that have only three neighbours. Three non-coplanar differences determine a three-vector exactly, so a tet missing one face is fully determined. The same shape as 2D, where two neighbours sufficed: the margin is one face, not zero. And the deficient ones read exactly 1.000, which is the zero again — a refusal, not a measurement.
