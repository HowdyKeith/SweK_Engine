📈 SSIM Compare — the local map, and what a whole-image number cannot say

render/perceptual.mjs's ssim() is the global form: one window over the whole image. render/ssimWindowed.mjs is SSIM as Wang et al. define it — an 11×11 Gaussian window slid across the image, producing a local map. This page runs both on the same pair and draws the map.

THE MAP IS THE POINT. The two numbers above it are both whole-image statistics and neither can say where. A metric that says where turns a red gate into a fixable bug report, which is the whole reason the module returns the map and its minimum rather than just the mean.

What the three numbers say

READ THE MEAN AND THE GLOBAL FIGURE AS THE SAME KIND OF THING. That is the finding this page exists to show: on a small local defect the windowed mean is nearly as blind as the global form, because averaging a local map over an image is still an average over the image. Switching a gate from one to the other would buy almost nothing. The worst window is what sees it, and it is the number that would start failing renders that pass today — which is why no gate has been switched to it yet.

NOT SHOWN HERE: whether the worst window is the right threshold for your renders. A single bad window can be a real defect or one legitimately noisy corner, and this page deliberately does not pick a cutoff — it shows you the distribution and where the worst one is, and the decision stays yours.
